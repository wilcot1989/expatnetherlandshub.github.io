**Short answer:** three things define the Turkish move. You need an MVV entry visa, which adds a consular stage that American and British movers skip. Your driving licence cannot be exchanged unless the 30% ruling applies to you. And uniquely in this cluster, the EU-Turkey Association Agreement gives you materially lower IND fees for family members — €85 rather than €254 — which is a rare structural advantage worth knowing about before you pay anything.

Turkey and the Netherlands have one of the longest-established migration relationships in Europe, and the Turkish-Dutch community is large and settled. That has an odd side effect: much of the advice circulating in Turkish-language expat groups describes rules as they were a decade or two ago. The four sections below are the ones where 2026 rules differ from folklore.

For the lifestyle and cost comparison see [Netherlands vs Turkey for expats](/guides/integration/netherlands-vs-turkey-expats-2026/). This guide covers the move.

## Your Timeline: Three Months Out to Month One

**Three months before.** Confirm the route. Employed work means the highly skilled migrant (kennismigrant) permit, filed by an employer that is an IND-recognised sponsor. Family reunification with a partner or spouse already in the Netherlands is the other common route, and is the one where the Association Agreement fee applies. Start collecting documents: birth certificate, marriage certificate, and where required a criminal record statement. Turkey is a party to the Apostille Convention, so an apostille from the competent Turkish authority normally replaces consular legalisation — confirm the requirement for your specific documents, because it varies by document and by the receiving Dutch authority. See [apostille and legalisation](/guides/legal/apostille-legalization-netherlands-2026/).

**Two months before.** Permit application filed, MVV appointment booked. Begin the housing search — see [finding housing](/guides/housing/finding-housing-netherlands-expats-2026/) and [rental scams](/guides/housing/rental-scams-netherlands-expats-2026/), because advance-payment scams target applicants who cannot view a property in person.

**One month before.** Collect the MVV. Plan the transfer of savings out of lira, and get written confirmation from your Turkish bank of what documentation a larger outbound transfer requires. Book the gemeente appointment as soon as you have an address — Amsterdam runs four to six weeks, smaller cities one to two.

**Arrival week.** Gemeente registration and [BSN](/guides/legal/bsn-registration-guide-2026/), residence card collection, a GGD tuberculosis appointment if the IND's country list applies to you, [DigiD](/guides/legal/digid-guide-expats-2026/).

**First month.** Dutch bank account, basisverzekering, and the 30% ruling application if you are on an employment route. Use the [first 30 days checklist](/guides/legal/first-30-days-netherlands-expat-checklist-2026/).

## Difference 1: The MVV Stage, and What the Association Agreement Changes

Turkey is not MVV-exempt, so your route is the combined application: the Dutch sponsor files for the MVV and the residence permit together, and you collect the sticker at a Dutch mission in Turkey. That is the same process an Indian or South African applicant follows, and it is the main reason a Turkish start date is harder to pin down than an American one.

Where the Turkish position is genuinely different is in the fees. The IND fee schedule prices a residence permit to stay with a family member or partner at €254 for most nationalities. For a Turkish family member it lists €85, with lower amounts again for children. That is not a rounding difference — for a family of four arriving on family-reunification permits it is a substantial saving, and it exists because of the EU-Turkey Association Agreement rather than because the IND chose to be generous.

Two caveats. First, the reduced fee attaches to the family route, not to everything: employment permits, the EU Blue Card and self-employment permits are charged at the standard €423. Second, fees are revised every 1 January, so verify the current amount on ind.nl before you pay.

There is a further, more technical strand of Association Agreement case law about the conditions that may be imposed on Turkish nationals — including standstill arguments used in self-employment and integration cases. It is real law, it has changed repeatedly through litigation, and it is not something to invoke from a blog post. If your case turns on it, use a Dutch immigration lawyer who works with Association Agreement cases.

**Salary criteria for the employment route.** For the period 1 July to 31 December 2026 the IND's gross monthly salary criteria for the highly skilled migrant permit, excluding the statutory 8% holiday allowance, are €5,942 for applicants aged 30 and over, €4,357 for under-30s, and €3,122 under the reduced criterion. Check the amount valid on your start date via ind.nl and the [highly skilled migrant guide](/guides/legal/highly-skilled-migrant-visa-netherlands-2026/).

## Difference 2: The Licence Problem, and the 30% Ruling Workaround

The RDW's list of non-EU/EFTA countries whose licences can be exchanged for a Dutch one runs, as of August 2026: Andorra, Alberta, Québec, Gibraltar, Great Britain, Guernsey, the Isle of Man, Israel, Japan, Jersey, Monaco, Northern Ireland, Singapore, Chinese Taipei, South Korea and the former Netherlands Antilles. Turkey is not on it, and the RDW is unambiguous that where the country is not listed, exchange is not possible.

The one documented route through is the expat scheme. In the RDW's own words: if you are in the Netherlands for work as a highly skilled migrant and the expat scheme applies to you, you may exchange your driving licence from any country. So for Turkish knowledge migrants the 30% ruling has a second, non-financial value — and if you are arriving on a family-reunification permit rather than an employment one, that route is closed and the CBR exam is your path.

**The deadline.** You may drive on your Turkish licence for 185 days after relocating. After that you need a Dutch licence. The CBR route means a theory exam, a practical exam and, realistically, lessons — several months and a four-figure budget. Read [exchanging your driving licence](/guides/daily-life/exchange-driving-license-netherlands-2026/) and the [CBR exam guide](/guides/daily-life/dutch-driving-license-cbr-guide-2026/), and start in month one rather than month five.

**On the 30% ruling itself:** nationality is irrelevant, geography is not. You must have been recruited from abroad and have lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for at least 16 of the 24 months before your Dutch employment started. All of Turkey clears the distance test easily, so the binding conditions are the recruitment requirement and the salary norm. File with the Belastingdienst within four months of your first working day to have it backdated. See the [eligibility guide](/guides/finance/30-percent-ruling-eligibility-guide-2026/).

## Difference 3: Diplomas, Professions and the IDW Step

Dutch employers in engineering, construction, logistics and IT hire from Turkey regularly and read Turkish CVs without difficulty. The formal recognition step becomes necessary in three situations: a regulated profession (healthcare, teaching, law and several technical certifications), admission to a Dutch degree programme, or an integration procedure.

IDW handles the credential evaluation — a comparison of your foreign diploma with the Dutch education system, stating the comparable Dutch level. Its published conditions matter for planning: the diploma must be nationally recognised, the programme at least 1,000 hours, one diploma per application, and IDW currently warns that processing times are longer than usual because of application volume. Work experience and short courses are not evaluated.

Practical advice from every client who has done this: get certified copies and official transcripts issued in Turkey before you leave, ideally with sworn translations. Obtaining them from abroad later is the single most complained-about administrative task in this move.

## Difference 4: The Lira Corridor

Turkish movers have two money problems at once, and they pull in opposite directions.

**Getting savings out.** Moving accumulated savings from lira into euros is not just a fee question — the exchange rate spread and the timing dominate the outcome, and the amounts involved are usually the largest single transaction of the whole move. Compare providers on the euro amount that arrives, not on the advertised fee, and ask your Turkish bank in advance what documentation an outbound transfer of your size requires so it does not stall at the counter.

**Sending money back.** Most Turkish expats I work with support family in Turkey, so there is an ongoing euro-to-lira flow in the other direction. Here the recurring spread matters more than any one-off fee: a small percentage difference on a monthly transfer compounds over years.

A multi-currency account also fixes the arrival-week sequencing problem, because you can open one before you leave and receive euros into a European IBAN while your Dutch bank application is still being processed — useful when a landlord wants a deposit before you have a BSN.

## Healthcare

No reciprocal arrangement covers you as a resident, so Turkish SGK cover does not follow you. Dutch basic health insurance is compulsory within four months of your municipal registration, backdated to the registration date if you sign up inside that window. Miss it and the CAK warns you, then fines you (around €469 in 2026 — check the current figure with the CAK), then assigns you a policy.

The model differences worth knowing in advance: you pay a monthly premium plus an annual deductible; the huisarts controls all specialist referrals; prescribing is markedly more conservative than in Turkish private hospitals; and adult dental care is not in the basic package. The basic package itself is identical at every insurer by law, so compare on premium, deductible and service. ## Two Turkey-Specific Items Worth Checking Early

**Military service.** Obligations for Turkish male citizens are governed by Turkish law and are unaffected by your Dutch residence. Deferment and other arrangements are handled through Turkish consular channels, and the rules have changed several times. Check your position with the Turkish consulate rather than with your employer's relocation agency.

**Nationality.** Dutch naturalisation applies a renunciation requirement with exceptions, which is why the Turkish blue card exists as a way of preserving defined rights in Turkey for former citizens. Whether an exception applies to you is individual. See the [Dutch nationality guide](/guides/legal/dutch-nationality-guide-expats-2026/) and take advice on both sides before applying.
