**Short answer:** three things define a Spanish move. There is no immigration process at all — you register at a municipality and receive a BSN. You almost certainly *can* claim the 30% ruling, despite the widespread belief that it is only for non-EU arrivals, because all of Spain sits outside the 150 kilometre exclusion zone that blocks most German applicants. And healthcare changes model rather than provider: you leave a tax-funded system that is free at the point of use for compulsory private insurance with a monthly premium and a deductible.

Spanish movers usually arrive for one of three reasons — a tech or aviation job in the Randstad or Eindhoven, an actively recruited healthcare role, or a research position. The administrative path is the easiest of any country in this cluster. The financial and healthcare adjustments are the ones that surprise people.

For the cost-of-living and culture comparison, see [Netherlands vs Spain for expats](/guides/integration/netherlands-vs-spain-expats-2026/). This guide is the move itself.

## Your Timeline: Three Months Out to Month One

**Three months before.** No visa process, so the timeline belongs to two things: housing, and — if you are in a regulated profession — recognition. Dutch rental supply in the Randstad is the genuine bottleneck of this move, and Dutch landlords expect a Dutch employment contract and often three to four times the rent in gross income. **Two months before.** Check your 30% ruling position and raise it with the employer before you agree the salary, because it changes your net pay substantially. Confirm the renewal status and expiry date of your Spanish driving licence.

**One month before.** Book the gemeente appointment the moment you have an address — Amsterdam four to six weeks, smaller cities one to two. ## Difference 1: Registration, Not Immigration

There is no IND application, no MVV, no sponsor and no permit. Free movement means the only mandatory step is registration in the BRP at your gemeente, legally within five days of moving in for stays over four months, which yields your BSN immediately at the appointment.

Two comparisons worth internalising. Your Indian or Turkish colleague waits weeks for a consular appointment and pays €423 for a permit; you pay nothing and wait only for a municipal calendar slot. But that colleague probably has the 30% ruling built into their package — and so, unusually for an EU mover, might you.

The IND does sell an optional verification against EU community law at €85 for an adult and €46 for a child under 18. It produces a document confirming lawful residence under EU law. It is not a permit and is not required to live or work here; occasionally a lender or a foreign authority asks for it.

**One Spanish-specific note:** the BSN is not the NIE. It looks similar in function but it is issued by the municipality as part of population registration, and there is no separate "foreigner number" step. If a Dutch form asks for a BSN, your NIE is not a substitute.

## Difference 2: You Probably Do Qualify for the 30% Ruling

I have had this conversation with more Spanish clients than with any other nationality: "the 30% ruling is for the Indians and Americans in my team, not for me." That is wrong, and it can be an expensive mistake.

The 30% facility lets a Dutch employer pay part of your salary as a tax-free allowance. Its conditions concern how and from where you were hired, not what passport you hold. The two that matter:

- **Recruited from abroad.** You must have been hired from outside the Netherlands rather than having found the job while already living here.
- **The 150 kilometre rule.** You must have lived more than 150 km from the nearest point of the Dutch border for at least 16 of the 24 calendar months before your Dutch employment started, measured in a straight line.

The whole of Spain, including the Canaries and the Balearics, is comfortably outside 150 km. That is precisely the test that disqualifies most arrivals from western Germany, Belgium and Luxembourg, and Spanish movers pass it without thinking about it.

Two caveats specific to Spanish movers. First, if you spent part of that 24-month window living in the Netherlands — an Erasmus year, an internship, a previous contract — those Dutch months count against you and you may fall below the 16-month threshold. Second, there is a salary norm for the ruling in addition to the geographic test. Work through the [30% ruling eligibility guide](/guides/finance/30-percent-ruling-eligibility-guide-2026/) and the [calculator guide](/guides/finance/30-percent-ruling-calculator-guide-2026/), and file the application with the Belastingdienst within four months of your first working day so that it is backdated.

## Difference 3: Healthcare Changes Model, Not Just Provider

This is the adjustment Spanish movers find hardest, and it is not about quality.

In Spain you have a tax-funded national system, free at the point of use, with a public health card. In the Netherlands, basic health insurance is compulsory and purchased from a private insurer: you pay a monthly premium yourself, plus an annual deductible (eigen risico) that applies to most care other than the GP, plus an income-related contribution collected through payroll. The obligation starts within four months of your municipal registration and is backdated to that date if you sign up inside the window; miss it and the CAK warns you, fines you (around €469 in 2026 — check the CAK for the current amount) and can assign you a policy.

What that means in practice:

- **The basic package is identical at every insurer**, defined by law. You compare premium, deductible and service, not coverage.
- **The huisarts is a strict gatekeeper.** No referral, no specialist — and Dutch prescribing is noticeably more conservative than Spanish practice. Paracetamol jokes exist for a reason.
- **Adult dental care is outside the basic package.** So is most physiotherapy beyond limited chronic indications.
- **Zorgtoeslag** is a healthcare allowance for lower incomes that offsets part of the premium — see [zorgtoeslag and huurtoeslag](/guides/finance/zorgtoeslag-huurtoeslag-expats-2026/).

Read [Dutch health insurance for expats](/guides/health/dutch-health-insurance-guide-expats-2026/), then compare premiums on <a href="https://go.expatnetherlandshub.com/independer-zorg?ref=/guides/legal/moving-to-netherlands-from-spain-2026/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener sponsored">Independer</a> once you have a BSN (affiliate link; we may earn a commission).

**If you keep a Spanish employer.** Living here while employed in Spain puts you into EU coordination territory, where which country insures you and collects your social contributions is decided by the rules, not by your address. Get it assessed by the SVB before cancelling anything.

## Difference 4: Qualifications, and Healthcare Recruitment in Particular

For most private-sector roles a Spanish degree needs no formalities. Two categories are different.

**Regulated professions.** The EU professional qualifications framework gives Spanish medical, nursing, pharmacy, dentistry, teaching and legal qualifications a defined recognition route between member states — a procedure rather than a rebuild. For healthcare, practising in the Netherlands requires BIG registration, and that includes a Dutch language requirement. Dutch hospitals and care providers recruit in Spain precisely because of staff shortages, and the better recruiters fund the language training as part of the package. The point to take away is sequencing: start the BIG process before you commit to a start date, because it commonly takes longer than the job search did.

**Where you need a level statement.** For a Dutch degree application, a public-sector role or an integration procedure, IDW issues credential evaluations comparing your qualification to the Dutch education system. Conditions: nationally recognised diploma, at least 1,000 hours, one diploma per application, and currently long processing times.

## Money: Euro to Euro, So Ignore the Currency Services

Worth stating plainly because most relocation content is monetised the other way: Spain and the Netherlands are both in the euro area, a SEPA transfer between them is free or near-free and arrives quickly, and there is no exchange rate to optimise. If something quotes you a fee to move euros from a Spanish account to a Dutch one, it is solving a problem you do not have.

The real money items in a Spanish move:

**Banking.** Your Spanish IBAN is legally valid here — refusing a valid EU IBAN is prohibited IBAN discrimination — but iDEAL needs a Dutch account, and Dutch payroll and landlord systems are not always cooperative with an ES IBAN. Open a Dutch account in your first weeks. The [best bank account guide](/guides/finance/best-bank-account-expats-netherlands-2026/) covers which accounts you can open before your BSN arrives.

**Tax residency.** Notify the AEAT of your change of residence, keep proof of when Dutch residence began, and expect the year of the move to require care. Spain applies its own residence tests and can assert residency for the year, which the Spain-Netherlands treaty then resolves — usually in your favour if your facts and documentation are clean. If you retain Spanish property or a substantial shareholding, get specific advice first. On the Dutch side, read the [Dutch tax system guide](/guides/finance/dutch-tax-system-expats-guide-2026/) and the [box 3 guide](/guides/finance/dutch-box-3-tax-savings-expats-2026/), because Spanish property and savings enter the Dutch wealth regime once you are resident here.

**Pension.** AOW, the Dutch state pension, accrues per year of Dutch residence, so yours starts building at registration. Insurance periods completed in other EU states are coordinated under EU rules for qualifying purposes; ask the INSS and the SVB for written statements rather than assuming. See the [Dutch pension guide](/guides/finance/dutch-pension-complete-guide-expats-2026/).

## The Smaller Practical Shocks

- **Housing is unfurnished, and "unfurnished" is literal.** Dutch *kaal* can mean no flooring and no light fittings. Budget for it.
- **Heating and insulation cost real money.** Energy is contracted separately from the tenancy — see [energy providers for expats](/guides/housing/energy-providers-expats-netherlands-2026/).
- **The working day ends at 17:00 and social life is diarised.** Spontaneity is not a Dutch feature; a *borrel* is scheduled three weeks out. See [Dutch work culture](/guides/work/dutch-work-culture-guide-expats-2026/) and [Dutch social etiquette](/guides/integration/dutch-social-etiquette-expats-2026/).
- **Language.** English works in most Randstad workplaces; Dutch is what you need at the gemeente, in a tenancy agreement and in a school portal. See [how to learn Dutch](/guides/integration/how-to-learn-dutch-complete-guide-2026/).

## The Mistake Spanish Movers Make Most Often

Not asking about the 30% ruling because they assume it does not apply to EU citizens. It is worth thousands of euros a year, the geographic test that disqualifies German arrivals does not touch Spain, and the application has a four-month deadline from your first working day for backdating. Raise it before you sign the contract, when the recruitment-from-abroad condition can still be evidenced cleanly.
