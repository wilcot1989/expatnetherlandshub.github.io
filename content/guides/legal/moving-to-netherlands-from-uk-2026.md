**Short answer:** the three things that define a British move in 2026 are that you need a residence permit but not an entry visa, that your driving licence is one of the few non-EU licences the RDW will still exchange without an exam, and that your UK financial life — bank, pension, NHS cover — needs decisions made before you change your address rather than after.

British movers sit in an odd middle position. Immigration-wise you are a third-country national like an American or an Indian applicant. Practically, several bilateral arrangements survived Brexit intact and make your move materially easier than theirs. Knowing which is which is the whole game.

This guide is the execution checklist. ## Your Timeline: Three Months Out to Month One

**Three months before.** Pin down the permit route. Employed work means the highly skilled migrant (kennismigrant) permit, filed by an employer who is an IND-recognised sponsor. Freelancing means the self-employment permit, assessed on a points system — see the [freelance visa guide](/guides/legal/freelance-visa-netherlands-2026/) and note that the well-known DAFT route is a treaty arrangement for US nationals, so do not plan around it without confirming your eligibility with the IND. Order any certificates you will need from the GRO with an apostille from the FCDO.

**Two months before.** Employer files with the IND. Start the housing search — this is the part that shocks British movers, because Dutch rental supply in the Randstad is tighter than anything in the UK outside central London. ## Difference 1: Third Country, But an Easy One

The immigration category is the unglamorous truth of Brexit: you are a third-country national, you cannot arrive and then convert from tourist status in most cases, and the 90-days-in-180 Schengen limit applies until you hold a permit.

What offsets it is the MVV exemption. The IND's exemption list includes the United Kingdom, so unlike applicants from India, Turkey or South Africa you do not have to collect an entry visa at a Dutch mission before flying. Your employer files the residence permit application, and once approved you travel and collect the residence card here.

**Salary criteria.** For the highly skilled migrant permit in the period 1 July to 31 December 2026, the IND's gross monthly salary criteria — excluding the statutory 8% holiday allowance — are €5,942 for those aged 30 and over, €4,357 for under-30s and €3,122 under the reduced criterion for recent graduates and former orientation-year permit holders. British salaries quoted in pounds convert unhelpfully here: what the IND tests is the euro figure in the Dutch contract, exclusive of holiday allowance. Check the current amount on ind.nl for your start date, and read the [highly skilled migrant guide](/guides/legal/highly-skilled-migrant-visa-netherlands-2026/).

**The 30% ruling.** Nationality is irrelevant; geography is not. The ruling requires that you lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for at least 16 of the 24 months before your Dutch employment started, and the entire UK clears that comfortably. The binding conditions for British movers are therefore the recruitment-from-abroad test and the salary norm. Apply within four months of your first working day to have it backdated — see the [eligibility guide](/guides/finance/30-percent-ruling-eligibility-guide-2026/).

## Difference 2: Your Licence Still Exchanges

The RDW maintains a short list of non-EU/EFTA countries whose licences can be exchanged for a Dutch one without any Dutch exam. As of August 2026 it includes Andorra, Alberta and Québec, Gibraltar, Great Britain, Guernsey, the Isle of Man, Israel, Japan, Jersey, Monaco, Northern Ireland, Singapore, Chinese Taipei, South Korea and the former Netherlands Antilles.

Great Britain and Northern Ireland both being on that list is the single most valuable survival from the pre-Brexit arrangements, and it is worth understanding how unusual it is. An American, Indian, Turkish or South African mover with no 30% ruling faces the full CBR theory and practical exam — months of preparation and a four-figure bill. You go to the gemeente with your licence and your BSN.

Two conditions to respect. You must have lived in the issuing country for 185 days in the year you obtained the licence, and the RDW cannot exchange an expired licence, so if your photocard is close to its renewal date, deal with the exchange promptly. You hand over the original UK licence, which goes back to the issuing authority — plan for that if you expect to return. Meanwhile, you may drive on the UK licence for the first 185 days after relocating. Details in the [licence exchange guide](/guides/daily-life/exchange-driving-license-netherlands-2026/).

## Difference 3: Healthcare Stops Being Free at the Point of Use

The NHS is a residence-based system. When you stop being a UK resident, you stop being entitled to it, and the GHIC in your wallet is a travel document for UK residents — it confers nothing in the country where you live.

The Dutch system replaces it with a compulsory private-insurer model funded by a monthly premium. Basisverzekering must be arranged within four months of your municipal registration; sign up inside the window and cover is backdated to your registration date. Miss it and the CAK warns you, then fines you (around €469 in 2026 — confirm the current amount with the CAK), then assigns you a policy.

Three adjustments British movers consistently underestimate:

- **You pay a monthly premium plus an annual deductible (eigen risico)** before most non-GP care is covered. GP visits themselves fall outside the deductible.
- **The huisarts is a strict gatekeeper.** No referral, no specialist. It is closer to the NHS model than to private care, and prescribing is notably more conservative than many Britons expect.
- **Adult dental care is not in the basic package.** Budget separately or take supplementary cover.

## Difference 4: Your UK Money Needs Decisions

British movers have more to unwind than EU movers, because sterling and the UK financial system do not follow you.

**The bank.** Several UK banks have restricted or closed accounts for EU-resident customers since Brexit, and the approach differs by bank and by product. Ask in writing before you change your registered address. If you still have UK income, a UK mortgage or a pension being paid in sterling, you want at least one working UK account.

**The pension.** Your accrued state pension entitlement is yours and is payable abroad. Two questions to put to gov.uk directly: whether annual uprating applies once payment goes to the Netherlands, and whether you can keep paying voluntary contributions to fill gaps. Both are individual to your record. For workplace pensions, ask the scheme what it allows for non-UK residents before you initiate anything — and be extremely sceptical of anyone offering to "transfer your UK pension" as a product. Our [Dutch pension guide](/guides/finance/dutch-pension-complete-guide-expats-2026/) covers what you start building here.

**The tax year mismatch.** The UK tax year runs to 5 April; the Dutch one is the calendar year. Your first Dutch return will cover a partial year and interact with a UK split-year treatment. It is the one year worth paying an adviser for.

## The Mistake British Movers Make Most Often

Assuming that because the culture, the language exposure and the flight time all feel familiar, the administration will be too. It is not the same system with different spellings. There is no equivalent of "just turning up" — you register with a municipality, you get a number that unlocks everything else, you insure yourself privately by law, and the sequence is unforgiving of steps taken out of order.

Do the licence exchange and the health insurance in your first month, not your fifth. Both have deadlines with real consequences.
