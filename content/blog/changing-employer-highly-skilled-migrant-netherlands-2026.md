A job move on a Dutch highly skilled migrant permit is two processes wearing one coat. There is the employment side — resignation, notice period, start date — and the immigration side, where your right to be in the country is attached to a specific recognised sponsor and has to be re-attached to a different one. Most people handle the first part carefully and assume the second will follow; the details that decide whether it goes smoothly are all in the second.

Every claim here was verified on ind.nl on **20 August 2026**. Rules change and salary amounts are revised every 1 January — use this to ask better questions, then confirm with the IND or an immigration lawyer.

> **Short answer:** Only an IND-recognised employer can apply for your permit, so check the new employer in the IND public register before signing. For a notification of change of employer submitted from 22 May 2026, IND applies a legal decision period of **45 days**, extendable by 15 days; if IND does not decide in time you may work for the new employer for the rest of your current permit's validity. The salary criterion is the amount applicable **on the date your new contract commences** — on 20 August 2026 that is EUR 3,122.00 reduced, EUR 4,357.00 under 30, EUR 5,942.00 from 30, gross per month, revised each 1 January. Holiday allowance does not count towards it. *All verified on ind.nl, 20 August 2026 — verify with IND, rules change.*

## First: Is the New Employer a Recognised Sponsor?

Ask this before the second interview, not after the offer.

IND states it plainly: only an employer recognised by the IND can apply for your permit. Every recognised sponsor appears in a **public register** on the IND website — around ten thousand companies, per the IND's own background article. A start-up that has never hired a non-EU national may need to apply for recognition first, which is its own procedure with its own decision period (ind.nl lists 3 months).

"Are you a recognised sponsor with the IND?" is a normal question in the Dutch market. For employers who routinely hire internationals, see the [best job boards guide](/blog/best-job-boards-netherlands-expats-2026/) and the [companies hiring internationals guide](/guides/work/companies-hiring-internationals-netherlands-2026/).

## The Decision Periods, Precisely

Per ind.nl's decision periods page, for a **notification of change of employer** where your current permit is still valid and you hold a permit for paid employment, work as a highly skilled migrant, the European Blue Card or research:

> A legal decision period of 45 days applies to applications submitted from 22 May 2026. In special cases, the IND may extend this period by 15 days.

And crucially:

> What if IND does not decide within this decision period? Then you are allowed to work for the new employer for the rest of the validity period of your current permit.

That last sentence is the safety net people do not know exists. It does not license starting before the notification is made — it protects you when the IND is slow.

**If you also request an extension**, ind.nl sets out two clocks: 45 days while your permit is still valid, and 90 days for the extension. Meet the requirements and the IND grants the permit for the duration of the contract with the new employer. A **first** highly skilled migrant application carries 90 days, though the IND notes recognised-sponsor applications are usually processed within a couple of weeks — plan against the legal maximum.

## The Salary Criterion Moves With You

Here is the detail that catches experienced people out. IND states that the required amounts change every year on 1 January, and for a job move:

> Does the highly skilled migrant change employers? The highly skilled migrant must then meet the required amount applicable on the date on which the employment contract with the new employer commences.

So the figure that matters is the one in force on your **new start date**. Negotiating in October for a January start? Negotiate against the January figure.

The amounts published on ind.nl for 2026, gross per month, for a change of employer:

| Category | Amount when changing employer |
|---|---|
| Reduced salary criterion | EUR 3,122.00 |
| Younger than 30 | EUR 4,357.00 |
| 30 years or older | EUR 5,942.00 |
| European Blue Card (reduced: EUR 4,754.00) | EUR 5,942.00 |

**The under-30 rule has a wrinkle.** For extension with the same employer, ind.nl notes the under-30 amount also applies once the migrant has turned 30 — but if they changed employers since the first permit, that change must have happened while they were still under 30. For a change of employer, ind.nl gives both cases: still under 30, EUR 4,357.00; now 30 or older, EUR 5,942.00.

**The reduced criterion** applies, per ind.nl, when the permit is applied for during an orientation year permit; when the person no longer has one but did have it; or within 3 years of graduation, the doctoral defence or the expiry of a research permit. If you came through the zoekjaar (see the [orientation year visa guide](/blog/orientation-year-visa-netherlands-2026/)), check whether it still covers your new start date.

## What Counts as Salary — and What Does Not

The criterion is not simply the number at the top of the offer letter. Per ind.nl:

**Counted:** reimbursements of expenses and fixed allowances such as a thirteenth month — provided they are in the contract, the employer transfers them to a bank account in the migrant's own name, and transfers them each month. A thirteenth month must therefore be paid monthly in 12 equal amounts to count.

**Not counted:** holiday allowance, the value of payment made in kind, and uncertain, non-regular pay elements such as overtime allowances, tips and payments from funds. So two offers with the same total package can land on different sides of the threshold. IND also requires the salary to be in conformity with the market and can ask the UWV for a recommendation.

When benchmarking, use the [average salary guide](/guides/work/average-salary-netherlands-2026/), the [salary comparison tool](/salary-comparison/) and the [gross-to-net calculator](/blog/dutch-salary-calculator-gross-to-net-2026/). And do the net calculation before accepting: the 30% ruling is often the largest variable in take-home pay and does not automatically travel between employers — see the [30% ruling changes guide](/blog/30-percent-ruling-extension-2024-changes-2026/) and the [30% ruling calculator](/tools/30-percent-ruling-calculator/).

## Notification Duties and the Gap Question

Per ind.nl's sponsor obligations page, a sponsor has an **obligation to provide information**: they must notify the IND of changes that have consequences for the residence permit, **mostly within 4 weeks**, using the IND notification forms or the Business Portal — and IND's examples include a foreign national who has stopped their work. So your outgoing employer reports that the employment ended, and your incoming employer handles the change. Neither is your paperwork, and both are your problem if not done.

**On unemployment between jobs:** the IND applies a search period for a highly skilled migrant who becomes unemployed, and it cannot be longer than the remaining validity of the residence permit. IND also states that where the requirements are no longer met — including where a contract is terminated prematurely — it will withdraw the permit. I am deliberately not printing a number of months: those rules have been amended recently and the outcome depends on your exact dates.

**The safe pattern:** signed contract with the new recognised sponsor first, notification filed, no unpaid gap. See the [Dutch employment contract guide](/guides/work/dutch-employment-contract-explained-2026/) and the [employment law basics guide](/guides/legal/dutch-employment-law-basics-expats-2026/) for the notice-period side.

## Practical Sequence for a Clean Move

1. **Check the register** before the offer stage.
2. **Fix the start date deliberately** — the criterion is the amount applicable when the new contract commences.
3. **Price the offer against the right threshold**, counting only fixed, contractual, monthly-paid elements.
4. **Get HR to confirm in writing who files what**, and whether an extension is needed too.
5. **Avoid an unpaid gap**, and re-run the 30% ruling question — it is not automatically portable.
6. **Watch your inbox** if IND post goes to your [Berichtenbox](/blog/mijnoverheid-berichtenbox-explained-expats-2026/).

## My Final Thoughts

The Dutch scheme is genuinely good at job mobility: a 45-day decision period, a fallback that lets you keep working if the IND is slow, and a public register you can check before applying. What turns it into a problem is sequence — resigning before the new employer is confirmed as a recognised sponsor, or agreeing a salary against last year's threshold for a January start.

So: check the register, fix the start date, price against the right number, get the filing responsibilities in writing — and **verify with the IND**. Everything above was checked on ind.nl on 20 August 2026, the amounts are revised every 1 January, and the search-period rules in particular have been moving.
