Permit renewal is the piece of Dutch immigration admin that feels routine right up to the moment it is not. The card in your wallet has an expiry date, the process for replacing it is mostly mechanical, and the failure mode is nearly always the same: someone starts too late, or assumed their employer had started, and ends up with a gap in their residence history that quietly pushes back permanent residency.

Every procedural claim below was checked on ind.nl on **20 August 2026**. Rules and fees change — the IND revises fees every 1 January — so verify the details on ind.nl for your own permit type, and get professional advice if your situation is unusual.

> **Short answer:** You can apply to extend a regular temporary residence permit when it expires within 3 months — not earlier. If it has just expired, apply within 4 weeks so the permits succeed each other and you avoid a residence gap, which IND says makes permanent residency and naturalisation take longer; after 2 years you cannot extend at all. IND must decide within 90 days (6 months for permanent residence). On a highly skilled migrant, researcher or higher-education student permit, only your recognised sponsor can submit. Fees on 20 August 2026: EUR 423.00 (highly skilled migrant extension), EUR 254.00 (family, study, first permanent permit), EUR 85.00 (child under 18). *All verified on ind.nl, 20 August 2026 — check ind.nl before you act, rules and fees change.*

## The Timing Rule That Trips People Up

The window is narrower than most people expect, at both ends.

**You cannot apply too early.** IND is explicit: you apply for extension because your permit expires *within 3 months*. If it is still valid for longer, you cannot yet submit, because the IND is then unable to make a proper assessment.

**You should not apply late.** IND's own framing is "extend in time, prevent a residence gap": apply while the permit is still valid. If validity has just expired, submit no later than **4 weeks** after expiry so the permits succeed each other. **After 4 weeks**, apply as soon as possible, expect a possible residence gap, and include a letter explaining the delay. **After 2 years** the door closes — you must apply for a new permit, which may mean returning to your own country for an MVV.

So: set a calendar reminder four months before the expiry date, and submit inside the three-month window with documents ready.

---

## Why a Residence Gap Is the Real Risk

A gap of a few weeks does not get you removed from the country. What it does is more insidious: IND states plainly that if you have a residence gap, it will take longer before you can apply for a permanent residence permit or become a Dutch citizen. Both routes rest on continuous lawful residence, and a hole in the sequence is exactly what the assessment looks for. Nobody notices a gap in year two; everybody notices it in year five. ## Who Actually Submits It

The second big failure mode is structural rather than personal.

IND states you can usually apply for the extension yourself. But if you hold a permit as a **highly skilled migrant, a researcher, or a student in higher education**, only your recognised sponsor can submit the application, via the Business Portal.

On a kennismigrant permit, in other words, your employer is the applicant, and you cannot fix a late submission by doing it yourself. So the item on your calendar four months out is not "apply" — it is "confirm in writing with HR who is submitting my extension and when". If your employer is unresponsive, or the relationship has broken down while your permit runs out, get independent help rather than hope: the [English-speaking lawyers guide](/blog/best-english-speaking-lawyers-netherlands-2026/) covers immigration specialists, and [relocation service providers](/guides/legal/expat-relocation-services-netherlands-2026/) often handle permit administration.

## Requirements, and the Permits You Cannot Extend

IND lists the conditions: a valid permit that can still be extended; you still meet the requirements of your own permit type; a valid passport or travel document; your main residence in the Netherlands, meaning you live here and spend most of your time here; you are not a danger to public order or national security; and you did not give or withhold false information in your last application.

**Cannot be extended at all**, per IND: Working Holiday Program or Scheme, cultural exchange, au pair, stay as a trainee or student on work placement, and the orientation year Netherlands. The zoekjaar therefore has no extension route — the transition is to another purpose of stay, typically the highly skilled migrant permit, and must be arranged before the year runs out. See the [orientation year visa guide](/blog/orientation-year-visa-netherlands-2026/).

Two conditions carry hidden traps. **Main residence in the Netherlands** is a real test for people who spend long stretches working abroad. And **still meeting the requirements** is where a changed job, a reduced salary or a relationship breakdown surfaces.

## Documents

IND does not publish one universal list, because it depends on your purpose of residence. What it does say: the written application form lists which documents you need, collect them all before applying, and official foreign documents must be **legalised and translated into Dutch, English, French or German**.

Legalisation and certified translation are the slow steps, so start any document you need from abroad when you set the calendar reminder, not in the month you apply. The [apostille and legalisation guide](/guides/legal/apostille-legalization-netherlands-2026/) explains the chain. Extensions are rarely refused on substance; they are delayed on paperwork.

## What Happens After You Apply

IND must decide within **90 days**, can extend that if deciding takes longer — for example because the application is incomplete — and will write to you if it does. For a permanent residence permit or long-term EU resident status the period is **6 months**.

IND points you to My IND to follow the case, and offers to send its post digitally to your Berichtenbox in MijnOverheid. If you take that option, switch on email notifications — the [MijnOverheid guide](/blog/mijnoverheid-berichtenbox-explained-expats-2026/) explains why that setting is the whole game.

A negative decision arrives as a letter and IND states you can object; the letter states the term, which is short, so read it the day it arrives. A new document is collected at an IND desk, and appointments at busy locations book out.

## A Renewal Timeline That Works

- **4 months before expiry:** confirm in writing who submits — you or your recognised sponsor. Check passport validity.
- **3–4 months before:** order any foreign documents needing legalisation or certified translation.
- **Inside the 3-month window:** submit, pay, save the confirmation.
- **While pending:** keep your BRP address current; check My IND or the Berichtenbox weekly.
- **On decision:** book collection immediately and check the dates on the new card.

If your circumstances change while an application is pending, seek advice quickly — the extension is assessed against the situation as it stands. Where a renewal collides with a job move, see [changing employers on a highly skilled migrant permit](/blog/changing-employer-highly-skilled-migrant-netherlands-2026/).

## My Final Thoughts

Renewal is administrative, not adversarial. Almost nobody in a stable situation is refused. What people lose is time — through a gap that delays permanent residency, documents that take six weeks to legalise, or an employer who submitted three weeks late. Two habits remove nearly all of that risk: a calendar reminder four months out, and written confirmation of who is submitting.

And the standard caveat, which I mean literally: **verify with the IND**. The rules here were checked on ind.nl on 20 August 2026, immigration policy changes, and your permit type may have conditions this article does not cover.
