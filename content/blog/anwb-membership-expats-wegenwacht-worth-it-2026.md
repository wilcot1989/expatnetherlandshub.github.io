Every expat who buys a car in the Netherlands eventually meets the same suggestion, usually from a Dutch colleague and usually phrased as if it were obvious: *you should join the ANWB.*

The ANWB is the Dutch motoring and travel organisation — roughly the counterpart of the AA in the UK or the ADAC in Germany. Its roadside assistance service, Wegenwacht, is genuinely well established and its yellow vans are a fixture of Dutch hard shoulders. But "you should join the ANWB" is advice, not analysis, and for a meaningful number of expats it is advice to buy something they already own.

This article puts real 2026 prices next to the alternatives so you can decide rather than default.

## What Wegenwacht Actually Costs in 2026

ANWB publishes its tariffs openly. There are three geographic scopes, each in three tiers, giving nine prices.

| Package | Instap | Standaard | Compleet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nederland** | EUR 74.25 | EUR 122.25 | EUR 152.40 |
| **Europa** | EUR 108.50 | EUR 184.75 | EUR 243.65 |
| **Europa Plus** | EUR 186.50 | EUR 262.75 | EUR 321.65 |

All figures are per year, from ANWB's own 2026 tariff page, and ANWB states that the Wegenwacht Service tariffs include ANWB membership. Wegenwacht applies to privately used cars, motorcycles, mopeds and vans up to 3,500 kg — not to vehicles in commercial use, which have a separate business tariff list.

Two things to notice before comparing.

**The geographic axis costs more than the tier axis.** Moving from Nederland Standaard to Europa Standaard adds EUR 62.50 a year; moving from Nederland Instap to Nederland Compleet adds EUR 78.15. If you never drive outside the Netherlands, the Europa packages are money spent on a scenario that will not occur — and if you drive abroad once a year, single-trip breakdown cover or the pechhulp module on a travel or motor policy may be the cheaper route than a year-round European subscription.

**The tiers differ in what happens *after* the roadside visit.** The higher tiers broaden replacement transport, assistance at your own address, and what happens when the fault cannot be fixed on the spot. ANWB's tariff page gives the prices and refers to the individual package pages for the coverage detail, so read the package page for the tier you are considering rather than assuming Compleet is simply "more".

## The Question That Decides It: What Do You Already Have?

This is the step most people skip, and it is the one that most often changes the answer.

**If you have a private lease**, breakdown assistance is typically included in the monthly price along with insurance, road tax, maintenance and tyres. Buying Wegenwacht on top of a lease that already includes pechhulp is paying twice for the same van. Check the contract's service annex; details on what lease bundles are in [car leasing options for expats](/blog/car-leasing-options-expats-netherlands-2026/).

**If you have a Dutch motor policy**, check whether pechhulp is included or available as a module. Several Dutch insurers offer it, and as an add-on to an existing policy it is generally cheaper than a standalone subscription for Netherlands-only cover. Legal assistance and replacement transport are frequently bundled in the same place.

**If you have a company car**, the employer or the leasing company almost always arranges breakdown assistance. Ask for the card or the number, and confirm whether it covers private use and family members driving the car.

**If you have travel insurance or a premium bank card**, European breakdown or repatriation cover is sometimes included for trips. This rarely replaces domestic cover but often replaces the reason people buy the Europa tiers.

Only if all four come back negative are you genuinely buying breakdown cover from scratch — and that is the situation in which ANWB's entry tier looks reasonable.

## The Alternatives in 2026

The Dutch breakdown market changed recently. Route Mobiel, for years the standard price challenger to ANWB, has ceased operating. That leaves three realistic routes.

**1. A module on your car insurance.** Usually the cheapest way to get Netherlands-wide breakdown cover if you already hold a motor policy, because it rides on an existing contract. The trade-off is that the service is delivered by whichever assistance partner the insurer uses, and the conditions can be narrower on home-start and replacement transport.

## When Wegenwacht Is Worth It for an Expat

The case is strongest when several of these are true:

- **Your car is older.** Breakdown probability rises with age, and older cars are also the ones for which a recovery bill is a large share of the vehicle's value.
- **You have no breakdown cover anywhere else.** No lease, no insurer module, no company car.
- **You drive regularly outside the Netherlands.** Not once a year — regularly. Cross-border cover is where the ANWB proposition is most differentiated and hardest to assemble cheaply elsewhere.
- **You cannot easily improvise.** A driver with limited Dutch, no local garage relationship and no colleague within reach is exactly who a single national number serves well. This is an underrated argument for new arrivals specifically.
- **You depend on the car daily.** Replacement transport in the higher tiers stops a breakdown becoming a missed working week.

## When to Skip It

- **Your lease or insurance already includes pechhulp.** Skip. This is the most common case.
- **You drive a nearly new car under manufacturer warranty.** Many manufacturers include roadside assistance for the warranty period. Check the handbook before buying anything.
- **You drive very little.** A car doing 5,000 kilometres a year within one province is a small exposure, and a one-off recovery paid out of pocket may cost less than years of subscription.
- **You do not own a car at all.** ANWB membership is sold to expats as a general "useful in the Netherlands" purchase. If you cycle and take the train, the motoring services are not the reason to join.

## How This Fits the Rest of Your Car Costs

Breakdown cover is a small line in a Dutch car budget, and it is worth keeping it in proportion. Nibud calculates average total monthly costs for a petrol car of EUR 370 for the mini class, EUR 432 for the compact class, EUR 572 for the small mid class and EUR 724 for the mid class, assuming ten years of ownership and 8,500 to 11,000 kilometres a year. Depreciation is the largest component of those figures; insurance is a modest share; breakdown cover at EUR 74 to EUR 152 a year is a rounding error by comparison.

The practical implication is that this is not a decision to agonise over on price. It is a decision to get right on *duplication*. Paying EUR 122 for Wegenwacht you already have through a lease is worse than paying EUR 152 for the tier you actually need.

## Practical Notes for New Arrivals

**Language.** ANWB is a Dutch organisation and its written material is primarily in Dutch. English-language help is available in practice for roadside assistance, but if you want to read the conditions yourself, allow time for that or ask for confirmation in writing.

**Registration and payment.** Expect the usual Dutch requirements: an address, and a Dutch IBAN for direct debit. If your money still arrives from abroad, move it into a Dutch account first — the banking setup is covered in [best banking for expats in the Netherlands](/blog/best-banking-expats-netherlands-2026/).

**Keep the number reachable offline.** Save the assistance number in your phone and write it somewhere in the car. A breakdown is a poor moment to discover you cannot remember which provider you signed up with — a genuine risk if your cover came bundled with a lease or a policy rather than as a card in your wallet.

**Check the vehicle scope.** Wegenwacht covers privately used cars, motorcycles, mopeds and vans up to 3,500 kg. If you use the vehicle commercially — as a ZZP courier, for example — the business tariffs apply instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does ANWB Wegenwacht cost in 2026?**

From EUR 74.25 a year (Nederland Instap) to EUR 321.65 (Europa Plus Compleet), per ANWB's 2026 tariff page. Netherlands: EUR 74.25 / EUR 122.25 / EUR 152.40. Europa: EUR 108.50 / EUR 184.75 / EUR 243.65. Europa Plus: EUR 186.50 / EUR 262.75 / EUR 321.65.

**Is membership separate from Wegenwacht?**

ANWB states that the Wegenwacht Service tariffs include ANWB membership, so in practice you buy them together.

**Is it worth it?**

Only after you have checked what you already have. With no other breakdown cover, an older car, or regular driving abroad, the case is good. With a lease or an insurer module that already includes pechhulp, it is a duplicate.

**Do I need it if my insurance includes pechhulp?**

Generally no. Duplicated breakdown cover is one of the most common avoidable overlaps in a Dutch insurance set.

**Does ANWB help in English?**

English-language service is available for roadside assistance; written material is primarily Dutch. Ask for written confirmation of anything you cannot read yourself.

**Are there cheaper alternatives?**

Yes — insurer pechhulp modules and smaller standalone providers, generally below ANWB's mid and upper tiers. Route Mobiel, the best-known former challenger, has stopped operating. Compare coverage scope, not only price.

**What do the higher tiers add?**

Mainly what happens after the roadside visit: replacement transport, help at your own address, and provisions when the fault cannot be fixed on the spot. Read the individual package page for the tier you are considering.

## Final Thoughts

Wegenwacht is a good product with transparent pricing, which makes this an unusually easy decision to get right — and an unusually easy one to get wrong by not asking a single question first.

So ask it: what breakdown cover do I already have, through my lease, my insurer, my employer or my manufacturer warranty? If the honest answer is none, ANWB's Nederland Instap tier at EUR 74.25 a year is a small, sensible purchase, and the Europa tiers make sense if you genuinely drive across borders. If the answer is that you already have it, the best version of this advice is to keep your money.
