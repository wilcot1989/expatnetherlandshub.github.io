Most expats in the Netherlands meet the Dutch tax system once a year, in spring, when the return is due. Homeowners and people with freelance income on the side tend to meet it twelve times a year instead — because they have a *voorlopige aanslag*, and their tax position is settled monthly rather than annually.

That difference is worth real money in cash-flow terms, and October is the right month to think about it, because the paperwork for the coming year opens in December. Get the request in early and payments start with the year; leave it and you spend eleven months lending the Belastingdienst money that is yours.

> **Short answer:** A voorlopige aanslag is a provisional tax assessment for the current year, settled in equal monthly instalments instead of one annual figure. Homeowners use it to receive mortgage interest relief monthly; freelancers use it to pay income tax monthly instead of facing a single bill. The Belastingdienst opens the form for the coming year at the start of December (for 2026, from 1 December 2025), continues an existing assessment automatically, pays the first instalment around eight weeks after the request, and pays later instalments on roughly the 15th of the month. Source: [Belastingdienst — voorlopige aanslag](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/voorlopige-aanslag/).

## What it is, mechanically

The Dutch income tax year is settled in arrears: you earn in 2026, file in spring 2027, and receive or pay the difference some months later. A provisional assessment short-circuits that lag by estimating the outcome in advance and settling it as you go. It runs in two directions.

**Money coming to you.** The classic case is *hypotheekrenteaftrek* — mortgage interest relief. If you own the home you live in, the interest is deductible and the deduction usually produces a refund. Rather than waiting until the following year, you can have the estimated refund paid monthly. For most homeowners this is the largest recurring tax effect in their finances.

**Money going out.** If you have income that is not taxed at source — freelance profit, box 3 income above the tax-free allowance — you can pay the estimated tax monthly instead of facing one large assessment later. It is not a discount but a smoothing mechanism, and it removes the classic freelancer trap of spending money that was always going to be tax.

The annual return remains the final word: whatever the assessment got wrong is corrected there.

## Who should have one

Four groups benefit most clearly:

**Homeowners.** If you bought this year, you are probably owed money and may not have set anything up. The mortgage adviser who arranged the loan sometimes handles the first request and sometimes assumes you will — confirm which happened. If you are still working out whether buying is realistic, the [expat mortgage guide](/guides/finance/dutch-mortgage-explained-expats-2026/), the [how much can you borrow](/blog/how-to-get-a-mortgage-as-expat-netherlands-2026/) breakdown and the [best mortgage broker for expats](/blog/best-mortgage-broker-for-expats-netherlands-2026/) comparison cover the decision.

**Freelancers and ZZP'ers.** A quarterly VAT rhythm plus an annual income tax bill is the worst of both worlds for cash flow. A provisional assessment gives income tax the same monthly shape as everything else you pay — see [tax filing for freelance expats](/blog/tax-filing-freelance-expat-netherlands-2026/).

**People with substantial box 3 assets.** Above the tax-free allowance, the box 3 charge is predictable enough to pay monthly. What it is based on, and what changes in the years ahead, is in [box 3 in 2027](/blog/box-3-tax-netherlands-2027-expats/).

**Anyone whose income changed sharply mid-year.** Payroll withholding assumes a stable year, and a stable year is exactly what a move to the Netherlands is not.

If none of those apply — one Dutch employer, rented flat, no side income — you almost certainly do not need one.

## How to request or change one

**Requesting.** Log in to Mijn Belastingdienst with your DigiD and open the Voorlopige aanslag form for the relevant year. You enter estimates: expected salary, mortgage interest paid, WOZ value for the *eigenwoningforfait*, expected profit if self-employed, assets on 1 January for box 3, and your fiscal partner's details.

**Timing.** The form for the coming year appears at the start of December — the Belastingdienst confirmed 1 December 2025 for the 2026 form. Request one later in the year and the annual amount is simply spread over fewer months, so the instalments are bigger.

**When money moves.** The first payment comes about eight weeks after the request, with later payments usually on the 15th of the month. Plan around that lag rather than expecting a December request to produce January money.

**Changing.** The same form changes an existing assessment. Do it when reality diverges from the estimate: you overpaid or refinanced the mortgage, sold the property, your partner started or stopped working, or your income changed materially.

**Stopping.** You can stop an assessment that pays out to you monthly — for example after selling the house. Do it as soon as the basis for the refund disappears, because every month it continues is a month you will have to repay.

## The expat-specific complications

**The 30% ruling changes the arithmetic.** The expat ruling lowers your taxable salary, and deductions are worth what they save you at the margin — so the same mortgage interest is worth less to someone with the ruling than to a colleague on the same gross without it. When the ruling starts, ends or steps down, the assumptions in your assessment go stale immediately. What changes from 2027 is in [the 30% ruling in 2027](/blog/30-percent-ruling-2027-changes-expats/).

**Fiscal partnership is not the same as being married abroad.** Whether you count as fiscal partners under Dutch rules determines how deductions are split, and getting it wrong is a common cause of a large correction later — see [finances for international couples](/blog/joint-accounts-netherlands-international-couples-2026/).

**Migration years break the estimate.** In the year you arrive or leave, part of the year sits outside Dutch residence, so an estimate built on a full Dutch year will be wrong — one of the few cases where paying an [adviser](/guides/finance/best-tax-advisors-expats-netherlands-2026/) for the first assessment clearly pays off.

**Automatic continuation is a trap as well as a convenience.** The Belastingdienst rolls last year's assessment forward and sends it around mid-December. Do nothing and it keeps paying — or collecting — on the old assumptions all year.

Suppose a household pays €9,000 in deductible mortgage interest and the *eigenwoningforfait* adds €2,200 back to taxable income, so the net deduction is €6,800. At an effective relief rate of 37%, the modelled annual benefit is about €2,516 — roughly €210 a month.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a voorlopige aanslag?

A provisional tax assessment for the current year, settled in equal monthly instalments. It pays you monthly if you are owed money (typically mortgage interest relief) or collects monthly if you owe (typically freelance or box 3 income).

### When can I request one for next year?

The form appears in Mijn Belastingdienst at the start of December — 1 December 2025 for the 2026 form. Existing assessments continue automatically, arriving around mid-December.

### When does the first payment arrive?

About eight weeks after the request, with later instalments usually on the 15th of the month. You do not need to reapply each year, but you do need to change it yourself when your mortgage, income, partner situation or ruling status changes.

### What if my estimate was too optimistic?

You repay the difference through the annual return, and interest may apply. Reviewing mid-year is cheaper than discovering the gap eighteen months later.

## My final thoughts

The voorlopige aanslag is the most underused piece of Dutch tax admin among internationals, for a simple reason: nobody tells you it exists. Homeowners in particular can spend two or three years waiting for an annual refund that could have arrived monthly the whole time.

The discipline that makes it work is unglamorous. Request or review it in December, when the form opens. Change it the same month your circumstances change, not the following spring. And if you own property, hold significant assets or have income the payroll never sees, treat it as part of your standard financial setup here — alongside the annual return itself, covered step by step in [how to file Dutch taxes as an expat](/blog/how-to-file-dutch-taxes-as-expat-2026/).
