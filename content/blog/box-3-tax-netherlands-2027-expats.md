Box 3 — the Dutch tax on savings and investments — has been the most unstable part of the tax system for years. Court rulings, repair legislation, replacement legislation and delays have made it almost impossible to plan around, and every autumn readers ask the same question in slightly different words: what happens next year, and how much will it cost me?

So this article does something narrow on purpose. It sets out what is confirmed, what is scheduled and what is not yet known, each with its source and verification date. Where a 2027 figure is not published, we say so instead of guessing.

> **Short answer:** As verified on 20 August 2026, 2027 is expected to be another year under the existing forfaitary box 3 system. The replacement law — the Wet werkelijk rendement box 3, which taxes actual returns — was passed by the Tweede Kamer on 12 February 2026 and by the Eerste Kamer on 7 July 2026, with an intended commencement date of **1 January 2028** ([rijksoverheid.nl timeline](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/inkomstenbelasting/plannen-werkelijk-rendement-box-3/tijdlijn-werkelijk-rendement-box-3); [Eerste Kamer dossier 36.748](https://www.eerstekamer.nl/wetsvoorstel/36748_wet_werkelijk_rendement_box)). The percentages and tax-free allowance for 2027 are set through the Belastingplan for that year, so check [belastingdienst.nl](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/box-3/) for the confirmed figures rather than relying on projections.

## How box 3 works right now

Box 3 does not tax your wealth directly, and it does not tax your actual income from that wealth. It taxes an *assumed* return on the value of your assets on 1 January, then applies a flat rate to it. Three moving parts: your asset mix on 1 January (different assumed-return percentages apply to bank deposits, to investments and other assets, and to debts), the tax-free allowance (*heffingsvrij vermogen*), which is deducted first and doubles for fiscal partners, and the box 3 rate applied to the result.

For 2026 the Belastingdienst publishes **1.28%** for bank deposits, **6.00%** for investments and other assets, **2.70%** for debts, a rate of **36%**, and a tax-free allowance of **€59,357** per person (€118,714 with a fiscal partner). The investments percentage is already fixed; the bank-deposit and debt percentages remain provisional until early 2027 ([berekening box 3-inkomen 2026](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/box-3/content/berekening-box-3-inkomen-2026); [heffingsvrij vermogen](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/box-3/content/heffingsvrij-vermogen), which lists €57,684 for 2025).

The structural problem is obvious once you see it: someone with a savings account earning 1% and someone with a portfolio that fell 10% are both taxed on an assumption. That is what took the system to the Supreme Court, and why the replacement law exists.

## What is confirmed for 2027

**The forfaitary system continues.** Because the Wet werkelijk rendement is aimed at 1 January 2028, 2027 remains a year of assumed-return percentages, a tax-free allowance and a flat rate.

**The 2027 numbers come from the Belastingplan for 2027.** Each year's percentages and allowance are fixed in the annual tax package presented on Prinsjesdag and then finalised by Parliament. As of the verification date — 20 August 2026 — the 2027 package had not been presented, so anyone quoting confident 2027 box 3 percentages is projecting, not reporting. When it is out, the figures appear on the Belastingdienst's [box 3 pages](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/box-3/), which is the source to trust over any secondary summary, including this one.

**The counter-evidence principle still matters.** Following Supreme Court rulings, taxpayers whose actual return was lower than the assumed return should be taxed on the actual return. In practice that is an evidence exercise: annual statements covering every account and asset for the full year.

## What changes in 2028, and why you should care now

The Wet werkelijk rendement box 3 shifts the basis from an assumed return to your real one. Broadly, recurring income — interest, dividends, rent — becomes taxable as it arises, while value changes on assets such as listed shares are taxed annually, with separate treatment for real estate. Given how much misinformation circulates here, read the government's own explanation at [rijksoverheid.nl](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/inkomstenbelasting/plannen-werkelijk-rendement-box-3).

The legislative history, verified on 20 August 2026 from the [official timeline](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/inkomstenbelasting/plannen-werkelijk-rendement-box-3/tijdlijn-werkelijk-rendement-box-3): the bill was submitted to the Tweede Kamer on **19 May 2025**, passed there on **12 February 2026**, debated in plenary by the Eerste Kamer on **30 June 2026** and adopted by it on **7 July 2026** ([dossier 36.748](https://www.eerstekamer.nl/wetsvoorstel/36748_wet_werkelijk_rendement_box)). Intended commencement is **1 January 2028**, with the actual date set by royal decree.

Two consequences follow for expat savers, and neither requires a 2027 figure.

**Record-keeping becomes the whole game.** A system based on actual returns needs actual numbers, per account, per year. If your assets sit across a Dutch bank, a foreign bank, an international broker and a crypto exchange, start collecting complete annual statements now rather than reconstructing 2027 from memory in 2029. The same discipline already helps under today's counter-evidence route.

**Asset category stops driving the outcome.** Under an assumed-return system, what an asset *is* determines the bill; under an actual-return system, what it earned does. That shifts the relative attractiveness of cash versus investments in a way nobody can quantify for you in advance — a reason to avoid dramatic restructuring on speculation.

## The expat angle: worldwide assets and treaties

If you are a Dutch tax resident, box 3 in principle looks at your worldwide assets: foreign savings accounts, an international brokerage account, cryptocurrency, and a flat you still own back home — subject to tax treaties, which commonly allocate taxing rights over foreign real estate to the country where it sits and provide relief in the Netherlands.

Three practical points cause the most trouble:

**Currency and valuation dates.** Box 3 works on values at a fixed reference date, and assets held in another currency have to be translated at that date whether the rate suited you or not. **Foreign property.** Do not assume it is either taxed twice or ignored: the treaty position decides, and it is country-specific.

**Dual filing obligations.** US citizens in particular may face reporting duties elsewhere on the same assets, separate from box 3.

## What to do this autumn

**Do not restructure on speculation.** No confirmed 2027 percentages existed at the verification date, and the structural change is a 2028 event.

**Fix your record-keeping.** One folder per year, an annual statement for every account and asset, foreign ones included. It serves the counter-evidence route now and the actual-return system later. And know your 1 January position, because under the current system the reference date drives the bill.

**Check the figures yourself when the tax package lands**, on the Belastingdienst [box 3 pages](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/box-3/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is box 3 changing in 2027?

Not structurally. The replacement law is aimed at 1 January 2028, so 2027 is expected to run on the existing forfaitary system.

### What are the current figures?

For 2026: 1.28% on bank deposits, 6.00% on investments and other assets, 2.70% on debts, a 36% rate, and a €59,357 tax-free allowance per person. The savings and debt percentages are provisional until early 2027.

### When will the 2027 figures be known?

They are set through the annual tax package and published by the Belastingdienst. On 20 August 2026 they were not yet available.

### Does box 3 cover assets abroad?

For Dutch tax residents, in principle yes, subject to treaties — which often treat foreign real estate differently. And if your actual return was lower than the assumed one, Supreme Court rulings established that the actual return should be the basis, provided you have documentary evidence for the full year.

## My final thoughts

Box 3 has been a moving target for so long that many expats have stopped trying to follow it. That leads to two expensive habits: paying an assumed-return bill that was higher than the real return without ever gathering the evidence, and making structural decisions on headlines about a law that has not commenced.

The calm version: 2027 looks like more of the same, with figures published in the ordinary way, and 2028 is the real change. What helps in between is unglamorous — complete annual records, knowing your position at the turn of each year, and proper advice before restructuring anything.
