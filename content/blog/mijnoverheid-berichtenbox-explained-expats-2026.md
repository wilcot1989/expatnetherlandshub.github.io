There is a piece of Dutch admin that almost no relocation checklist mentions and that quietly causes more missed deadlines than any other: your government post has probably stopped arriving on paper, and you may not have looked at where it went instead.

Every resident aged 14 or over has a MijnOverheid account. Inside it sits the Berichtenbox — a digital letterbox that connected government organisations use instead of the postal service. If you never activated the account, or activated it and never switched on email notifications, letters about your tax return, your child benefit or your car's inspection are being delivered to a room you never enter.

> **Short answer:** MijnOverheid is the government's personal portal: your data from the BRP, RDW and Kadaster in one place, plus the Berichtenbox for digital government post. Everyone aged 14 or over gets an account automatically, but you must activate it by logging in with DigiD, and it cannot be cancelled. Switch on email notifications immediately — nothing else alerts you to new messages — and install the Berichtenbox app. You can opt out per organisation and keep receiving paper, except for the Belastingdienst, which now sends some post digitally only. *Verified on rijksoverheid.nl and mijnoverheid.nl, 20 August 2026.*

## What MijnOverheid Contains

Two halves, serving different purposes.

**Uw gegevens — your data.** MijnOverheid shows what the government holds about you, your work, your AOW pension, your home, your car and your studies: personal data from the Basisregistratie Personen (BRP), vehicle data at the RDW, and the parcel or ownership data for your home at the Kadaster. It is the fastest way to check the state has your details right — and the only convenient way to notice when it does not.

**De Berichtenbox — the message box.** Your digital letterbox for post from connected government organisations, described by MijnOverheid with three typical examples: your tax return, child benefit, and passport renewal. Add the everyday ones: the RDW's notice that your car is due for its APK, decisions from Dienst Toeslagen, letters from your municipality.

Everyone aged 14 or over automatically receives an account, with two surprising consequences. You still have to **activate** it — go to mijnoverheid.nl and log in with your personal DigiD. And you **cannot cancel** it: the account exists whether you use it or not, which is precisely why the unused version is dangerous.

If you do not yet have DigiD, that is the prerequisite. The [DigiD guide for expats](/guides/legal/digid-guide-expats-2026/) covers the application, the activation letter and the app.

## The First Ten Minutes

Do these once, in order, and the system starts working for you instead of against you.

1. **Log in with DigiD and activate the account.** Use the DigiD app if you have it.
2. **Turn on email notification.** When a message arrives you can choose to receive an automatic email. This is the setting that turns a hidden inbox into a working one. Use an address you actually read.
3. **Install the Berichtenbox app**, available in the App Store and Google Play Store, so you can read government post on your phone.
4. **Review the organisations list** in your settings, and switch individual bodies off if you would rather have paper.
5. **Check your own data** while you are there: address, name spelling, marital status, nationality. Misspelt transliterated surnames are extremely common and they propagate — banks, insurers and employers all pull from the BRP.

## What Still Comes on Paper — and the Belastingdienst Exception

An organisation can only deliver to your Berichtenbox if it is connected to MijnOverheid. Bodies that are not connected still write to you conventionally, and plenty of important correspondence — health insurer, landlord, pension fund, bailiff — is not government post at all and never appears there.

You may also choose paper. If you do not want messages from one or more organisations in your Berichtenbox, you can change that and receive their post by ordinary mail.

**The one thing you cannot switch off is the Belastingdienst**, because the tax administration now sends some messages digitally only. Since December 2025, MijnOverheid does let you indicate how you prefer to receive Belastingdienst post, via Settings — Organisations Berichtenbox. Read that as: the tax office is on a one-way trip to digital, so assume anything time-critical from them lands in the Berichtenbox.

Which matters most in filing season, when invitations, provisional assessments and final assessments all travel this route. ## The Language Problem, Honestly

MijnOverheid has an English information page. The portal and its messages are in Dutch, and MijnOverheid notes that even the My SVB environment it links out to is Dutch, with only the child benefit application form available in English.

So build a workflow rather than hoping for a translation button:

- Read with your browser's translate function, but **verify every date and amount against the Dutch original**. Machine translation is reliable on prose and unreliable on exactly the thing you need.
- Learn the words that signal an obligation: *aanslag* (assessment), *beschikking* (formal decision), *bezwaar* (objection), *termijn* (deadline), *uiterlijk* (no later than), *herinnering* (reminder), *aanmaning* (formal demand).
- Download PDFs to your own drive — much easier to hand a professional a saved file than to log in together later.
- Know the default: objections to Dutch government decisions are generally due within **6 weeks** of the date on the decision. Worth memorising, because the Berichtenbox is where that clock starts.

## How Expats Specifically Get Caught Out

**The activation gap.** You had DigiD from day one for the gemeente and the tax office, but never activated MijnOverheid. Messages accumulate unseen, with no paper fallback from organisations that only send digitally.

**The notification gap.** Account activated, notifications off. Functionally identical, and much more common.

**The moving gap.** Government post follows the BRP, so the Berichtenbox keeps working after a move — but paper senders do not. Notify the municipality on time, then check MijnOverheid to confirm the new address has propagated. The [house moving checklist](/blog/house-moving-checklist-netherlands-expats-2026/) has the sequence.

**The leaving gap.** People emigrate, deregister and stop logging in while a final tax assessment or benefit reconciliation is still coming. Keep checking for at least a full tax cycle after you go — see the [leaving the Netherlands checklist](/guides/legal/leaving-netherlands-expat-checklist-2026/).

**The wrong-data gap.** Data comes from different organisations, so you correct it at the source: report errors via the Meldpunt Fouten in Overheidsregistraties, and go to your municipality for BRP details such as address or name.

## It Is Also Where Your IND Post Can Go

Worth flagging for anyone on a residence permit: the IND lets you receive its post digitally in your MijnOverheid Berichtenbox, and says so in its own application instructions, alongside following your case in My IND.

Whether you want immigration decisions in the same inbox as your waste levy is a personal call. The argument for it is speed — you see a decision the day it is issued. The argument against is that you must then actually read the inbox. If you are mid-application, pick one channel deliberately and check it weekly. For what those timelines look like, see [renewing your Dutch residence permit](/blog/renew-residence-permit-netherlands-2026/).

## A Sane Routine

- **Weekly** if anything is pending — an application, an objection, a benefit change: open the app.
- **Monthly** otherwise: clear new messages, save PDFs worth keeping.
- **Once a year, in February:** check your own data before filing season.
- **Whenever life changes** — move, new job, marriage, separation, a child: confirm the BRP data updated, because half the Dutch system reads from it.

And treat email notifications as something you verify rather than assume. People change email providers and forget which address the government has.

## My Final Thoughts

MijnOverheid is not an interesting product and it will win no design awards. It is the address where the Dutch state now expects to reach you, and the cost of ignoring it is asymmetric: nothing happens for months, and then a deadline you never saw has already passed.

Ten minutes, once. Activate the account, switch on email notifications, install the app, check your own data. Then go back to ignoring it — but with alerts on, which is the entire difference.
