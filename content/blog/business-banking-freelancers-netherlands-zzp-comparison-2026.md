There is no best business account for freelancers in the Netherlands. There is only the account that matches how you actually invoice.

That sounds like a dodge, so let me make it concrete. A translator with fifteen Dutch clients and a translator with fifteen American clients have almost nothing in common as banking customers. The first needs an NL IBAN, iDEAL and cheap SEPA. The second needs multi-currency receiving and honest exchange rates, and could pay double the monthly fee and still come out far ahead. Ranking accounts one to five hides that entirely.

## First, the Two Things That Are Not Optional

**A KVK registration comes before the account.** Every provider opens the account in the name of a registered business, so you need your Chamber of Commerce number or extract in hand. If you have not done that step, start with our [KVK registration guide for expats](/guides/work/kvk-registration-expat-guide-2026/).

**Separation is a practical necessity even where it is not a legal one.** An eenmanszaak has no separate legal personality, so nothing in Dutch law forces you to open a business account. A BV does need one, because the company is a distinct legal entity. But for a sole trader the real argument is administrative: every VAT return and every question from the Belastingdienst becomes materially harder when private and business transactions share one statement. Ask any Dutch bookkeeper and you will get the same answer.

## The Costs That Are Not the Monthly Fee

Freelancers routinely optimise the wrong line. In rough order of how much money it typically moves:

**Currency conversion.** If any meaningful share of your income arrives in a non-euro currency, the exchange margin dwarfs every monthly fee in this article. A 1.5% spread on €40,000 of foreign income is €600 a year; the gap between the cheapest and dearest account here is under €300.

**Per-transaction charges.** Traditional business packages often include a bundle of transactions and charge beyond it. A freelancer with many small payments can pay more at a "cheap" bank than at a dearer flat-rate one.

**Card and cash fees.** Business debit card issuance, replacement cards, extra cardholders and cash deposits are all chargeable at some providers and free at others.

**Your own time.** An account that reconciles cleanly into your bookkeeping, in a language you read fluently, saves hours every quarter. That is worth more than a few euros a month, and it is the argument for English-first providers even when they are not the cheapest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Am I legally required to have a business account as a ZZP'er?**
Not as an eenmanszaak — there is no separate legal personality, so no legal obligation. A BV does need an account in the company name. For sole traders the case is administrative rather than legal, and it is a strong one.

**What does a business account cost in 2026?**
bunq Business ranges from a free tier to €23.99 a month. Knab is €7 for an eenmanszaak and €11 for a BV, currently with twelve free months. Wise Business charges €60 once with no monthly fee. Traditional banks sit in the high single digits to mid-teens per month plus per-transaction charges.

**Can I use Wise Business as my only account?**
Many international freelancers do. Note that Wise is not a bank, so the deposit guarantee scheme does not apply, and its euro details are a Belgian IBAN rather than an NL one.

**Do I need a KVK number first?**
Yes. Every provider opens the account in the name of a registered business and asks for the KVK number or extract.

**Are business deposits covered by the deposit guarantee scheme?**
At a bank with a Dutch banking licence, yes — up to €100,000 per depositor per licence for most ordinary legal forms, aggregated with your other deposits at that licence. Electronic money institutions are outside the scheme.

**Can a Dutch client refuse a Belgian IBAN?**
They should not: EU rules prohibit refusing a euro SEPA payment based on the IBAN's country. A small number of Dutch payers still resist in practice, mostly public bodies and older systems.

**Is a bundled bookkeeping package worth it?**
Only if it replaces software you would buy anyway. Price the increment — roughly €16 a month in Knab's case — and check your accountant can work with it.

## The Short Version

Match the account to the invoicing, not to a ranking.

Dutch clients and euros only: take the cheapest NL IBAN that clears your transaction volume, and stop thinking about it. Foreign currency in the mix: Wise Business pays for itself on conversion alone, and pair it with a cheap NL account rather than choosing between them. A BV with staff, cash or credit needs: pay for a traditional bank and get the facilities you actually need.

My practical tip: whatever you open, open it before your first invoice goes out, not after. Changing the bank details on invoices already in a client's payment system is a small administrative misery that repeats for months.
