Ask five expats how much they could borrow and you will get five different answers — not because the rules differ, but because each lender applies them in its own way. The gap between the most and least generous assessment of the same file is largest exactly where expats sit: temporary contracts, the 30% ruling, foreign income, and permits with an end date.

This article explains what actually drives the number, so that when a broker gives you a maximum you understand where it came from. What it deliberately does not do is quote interest rates or income multipliers: both change constantly, and a number that looks authoritative in an article is the fastest way to build a bidding strategy on sand.

> **Short answer:** Your maximum Dutch mortgage is the lower of two ceilings. The income ceiling comes from the annual responsible-lending norms set by NIBUD, applied to your gross annual income and reduced by registered debts. The value ceiling is the property valuation — you can normally borrow up to 100% of the home's value, so purchase costs and any overbid come from savings. NHG is an optional government-backed guarantee: in 2026 the limit is €470,000 (€498,200 including energy-saving measures) with a one-off fee of 0.4% of the loan (nhg.nl, 8 October 2025). Whether the 30% ruling raises your maximum depends entirely on the lender, so get the calculation in writing.

## Ceiling one: what your income allows

Dutch lending is affordability-driven rather than multiplier-driven. Each year, the National Institute for Family Finance — NIBUD — publishes financing burden norms: the percentage of gross annual income that may be spent on mortgage costs, differentiated by income level and by the interest rate on the loan. Lenders apply those norms rather than a rule of thumb.

NHG describes the logic plainly on its English pages: an NHG-backed mortgage "meets the criteria for responsible lending and borrowing set by the National Institute for Family Finance in the Netherlands (NIBUD)", so that "after making the monthly mortgage payment you'll still have money left for other expenses like groceries, insurance and your savings account" (nhg.nl English summary, retrieved 20 August 2026).

Three consequences follow, and they matter more than any calculator output:

- **The interest rate is an input.** Because the norms are rate-dependent, the same salary supports a different loan when rates move — a maximum quoted in spring may not hold in autumn.
- **Registered debt bites hard.** Consumer credit, a car lease, a credit card limit or a phone on instalments can appear at the BKR credit bureau, and lenders deduct a monthly amount for it.
- **Both incomes count, in a specific way.** For couples the lower income is included at a set share rather than in full.

## Ceiling two: what the property allows

Even with a comfortable income, Dutch lending is capped at the value of the home. The valuation report (taxatierapport) sets your loan ceiling, and everything else comes out of your own pocket: transfer tax where applicable, notary costs, valuation, advice and arrangement fees, and the NHG guarantee fee if you choose NHG.

In a competitive market this is the constraint that trips up newcomers: if you bid above the valuation to win a property, the difference is not financeable. Ask your adviser for a written cost overview before you start viewing.

## Where NHG fits

NHG is a guarantee provided to the lender by a government-backed foundation, the Waarborgfonds Eigen Woningen. It is not insurance you claim on casually: NHG lists the specific circumstances as job loss, the end of a relationship, work disability, and the death of a partner (nhg.nl English summary, retrieved 20 August 2026).

For 2026 the published parameters are:

- NHG limit: **€470,000**, up from €450,000 in 2025
- With energy-saving measures: **€498,200**
- One-off guarantee fee (borgtochtprovisie): **0.4%** of the amount borrowed
- From 2026, a single NHG limit applies to **all property types**

(Source: nhg.nl press release "NHG-grens in 2026 vastgesteld op € 470.000", 8 October 2025.)

Lenders typically price NHG loans lower because their credit risk is reduced, so the fee is often recovered through the rate. Whether that trade-off works for you depends on the loan size and the rate difference offered on the day.

## The expat-specific variables

### The 30% ruling

This is the question I am asked most, and the honest answer is that there is no single market rule. Lenders differ in whether they assess your full gross salary or the taxable portion, and in how they handle the years after your ruling ends.

Dutch mortgage adviser Mister Mortgage states that the maximum mortgage amount is determined based on your annual income, "with the 30% ruling factored in for the period you're still eligible for it in the future", and that once the benefit expires your monthly payments rest on your salary alone (mistermortgage.nl, retrieved 20 August 2026). Other advisers describe lenders that assess taxable income only.

Two takeaways. Ask each lender or broker to show the income figure they used, not just the maximum. And sanity-check affordability for the period *after* the ruling ends, because your loan will outlive the benefit. Our [30% ruling calculator](/tools/30-percent-ruling-calculator/) and [salary after tax calculator](/tools/salary-after-tax-calculator/) show what it does to your net pay meanwhile.

### Contract type, permit and foreign income

Lenders assess income durability. A permanent contract is the smoothest path; fixed-term contracts, agency work and self-employment each have their own documentation route, and NHG maintains specific arrangements for flex workers, agency workers and entrepreneurs. A temporary residence permit narrows the field of willing lenders rather than closing it. Foreign or foreign-currency income is where policy varies most — expect requests for tax returns, employer statements and sometimes translations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What determines the maximum mortgage I can get in the Netherlands?

Two ceilings apply at once and the lower wins: an income-based affordability ceiling built on the NIBUD responsible-lending norms and reduced by registered debts, and a value-based ceiling set by the valuation.

### Does the 30% ruling increase my borrowing capacity?

It depends on the lender. Ask for the income figure used, in writing, and stress-test affordability for the years after the ruling ends.

### What is NHG and do I need it as an expat?

An optional government-backed guarantee to your lender, tied to NIBUD criteria. In 2026 the limit is €470,000 (€498,200 with energy-saving measures) and the one-off fee is 0.4% of the loan.

### Can I get a Dutch mortgage on a temporary contract or permit?

Frequently yes, with heavier documentation. Permit type narrows the lender field rather than blocking you.

### How much of my own money do I need on top of the mortgage?

Enough for purchase costs plus any amount you bid above the valuation. Get a written cost overview before bidding.

### Should I use an online calculator or a mortgage adviser?

Both, in that order — a calculator to set your search range, an adviser to produce a number you can safely bid with.

## Final thoughts

The most expensive mistake is treating a calculator output as a bidding budget. The number that matters is the one a lender will commit to for your contract, your permit and your ruling — and getting it costs one advice appointment.

Do the boring part first: reduce registered credit, know how much cash survives your move, and ask every adviser to show their working.
