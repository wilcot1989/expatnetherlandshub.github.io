Dutch child benefit is one of the few parts of the system that mostly works without you. You register a birth at the town hall, a letter arrives, you click through a form, and four times a year money appears.

Which is exactly why international families lose money on it. The parts that need attention lie outside the automatic path: arriving with children already born, the education condition at 16, a partner who works in another country, and the separate income-tested schemes plenty of eligible households never apply for. ## What Kinderbijslag Is

A contribution towards the cost of raising a child, paid by the Sociale Verzekeringsbank (SVB) for children under 18. Two features make it unusual: it is **not income-tested** — a kennismigrant consultant and a part-time shop assistant receive the same amount for a child of the same age — and it is **paid per quarter, in arrears**.

The amount depends on the child's age, in three bands, as shown on svb.nl on 20 August 2026:

| Child's age | 1st and 2nd quarters of 2026 | From the 3rd quarter of 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 years | EUR 295.07 | EUR 298.40 |
| 6 to 11 years | EUR 358.30 | EUR 362.35 |
| 12 to 17 years | EUR 421.53 | EUR 426.29 |

Check [the SVB amounts page](https://www.svb.nl/en/child-benefit/amounts-and-payment-dates/child-benefit-amounts) for current figures before budgeting from them.

**The age band changes on quarter boundaries, not birthdays.** The SVB assesses the situation on the first day of the relevant quarter. If your child had not yet turned 6 or 12 on that day, you receive the old amount for that quarter — parents query this regularly, and it is working as designed.

---

## Payment Dates: Always Behind

Child benefit is paid after the end of each quarter. The SVB's published dates for 2026: **2 January** (for Q4 2025), **1 April** (Q1), **1 July** (Q2) and **1 October** (Q3). The money for October to December 2026 therefore arrives in early January 2027.

The same quarter logic governs your first payment: born before or on the first day of a quarter, entitlement starts from that quarter; born after, from the next one. A baby born on 2 October generates entitlement from 1 January and a first payment in early April, so budget the first months without it.

## How You Actually Claim It

For a birth in the Netherlands the path is mostly automatic. You register the birth with your municipality (gemeente) — the trigger for everything else — and the municipality informs the SVB. The SVB then writes to you automatically, to the address where the child is registered as living, within 4 weeks of the birth registration, and you submit the claim online via My SVB.

If you already receive child benefit for another child and live in the Netherlands, you generally do not apply again — you automatically receive a letter showing your new amount. **If no letter arrives after 4 weeks**, do not wait: it is usually an address or registration mismatch, exactly what happens when a family has just moved.

**A language note.** The SVB states that My SVB is in Dutch and that only the child benefit application form is available in English. Keep the Dutch letters and check dates against the Dutch original.

**If you moved here with children already born**, you are outside the automatic path — there was no Dutch birth registration to trigger the letter. Register the family at the gemeente, then contact the SVB. ## The Four-Month Health Insurance Deadline

This is the item I most often see missed, precisely because it is not part of the child benefit process.

Per rijksoverheid.nl (verified 20 August 2026): children under 18 are covered within the basic health insurance free of charge alongside a parent — no premium, and the deductible does not apply to them. But you **must register the child with a health insurer before they are 4 months old**, and cover then applies from the date of birth. You may use your own insurer but are not obliged to. From the first month after your child turns 18, you start paying a premium, the deductible applies, and your child can apply for healthcare allowance. The SVB flags the same deadline in its FAQ.

Since you have four months and no premium consequence for the child either way, this is the natural moment to check whether your own policy is still right — which hospitals and maternity providers it has contracted, and whether the supplementary cover you pay for includes anything you now use.

## Kinderbijslag Is Not Kindgebonden Budget

Two schemes, two bodies, endless confusion. **Kinderbijslag** comes from the SVB, is not income-tested, and is a fixed amount per child per quarter by age band. **Kindgebonden budget (child budget)** is paid by Dienst Toeslagen, is income-dependent and is linked to your household situation; the SVB notes that Dienst Toeslagen pays the whole child budget to the parent who receives the child benefit, and that a separation normally affects it.

Then there is the third scheme most working parents need: **kinderopvangtoeslag**, the childcare allowance, also from Dienst Toeslagen and income-dependent — the largest amount of money in play for most dual-income expat families, and the one with the most reclaim risk if you do not adjust it when your income changes.

Because these are separate applications, families frequently receive one and not the others. ## The Expat-Specific Situations

**Your partner works in another country.** The SVB flags this directly: there may be consequences for your child benefit, because your partner may qualify for family benefits from the other country. Report the situation rather than waiting to be asked — cross-border families are the most common source of later corrections.

**Your child lives outside the Netherlands.** Possible in defined circumstances. For a 16- or 17-year-old abroad the SVB requires full-time education, study for a qualification giving a good chance of skilled work, a completed qualification, or inability to study through illness or disability.

**Foster, adopted and step-children.** All can give entitlement; adopted children qualify even if from outside the Netherlands. For foster children the SVB requires that nobody else gets child benefit for the child, that you pay for their upkeep, that you receive no fostering allowance, and that the child lives at home with you.

**Separation and co-parenting.** Benefit goes to the parent the child lives with. Co-parents can agree how to divide it, in writing or via a court ruling; without an agreement you each get half.

**Your child turns 16.** The education condition applies: study or training, a school-leaving qualification (HAVO or VWO diploma, or MBO at level 2 or higher), or an exemption. For families in international school systems, check with the SVB — see the [international school cost guide](/blog/international-school-netherlands-cost-2026/) and the [DUO student finance guide](/guides/finance/duo-student-finance-international-students-2026/).

**Report changes** — moving, separating, a child leaving education. Reporting beats a reclaim letter two years later.

## My Final Thoughts

For a family with a baby born here, child benefit takes about ten minutes of real work and then runs itself. The value of understanding it lies in the adjacent things that are not automatic: the health insurer registration inside four months, the income-tested schemes you apply for separately, the education condition at 16, and the cross-border complication if a partner works abroad.

So make the benefits audit your action item rather than the child benefit claim — the households leaving the most money unclaimed are the ones receiving kinderbijslag and nothing else.
