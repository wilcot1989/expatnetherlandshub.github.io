Most expats discover the AOW question in the wrong order. They hear that the Dutch pension system is one of the strongest in the world, assume the state pension is part of that strength, and only later find out it is built up year by year — and that the years before they arrived simply do not count.

It is not a trap and it is not obscure: SVB, the Dutch social insurance bank, publishes the mechanics in plain English. But the consequence deserves an afternoon rather than a footnote. An expat who arrives mid-career is not building a full Dutch state pension, and no amount of salary changes that.

> **Short answer:** AOW is built up at 2% for each insured year, over the 50-year period before you reach AOW pension age, so 50 insured years gives a full AOW pension (svb.nl, retrieved 20 August 2026). Everyone who lives or works in the Netherlands is insured as a general rule, with listed exceptions. Arrive mid-career and your AOW is reduced by 2% for every year you were not living or working here. Voluntary insurance can sometimes fill past or future gaps. Your AOW age depends on your date of birth and is only final for people born before 1 October 1964. Check your own insured years in My SVB with DigiD.

## The three pillars, in the order that matters for you

Dutch retirement income comes in three pillars, and expats weigh them differently from Dutch colleagues.

**Pillar one — AOW.** The state pension, administered by SVB, built up through residence and work rather than contributions you choose. This is the pillar where being an expat costs you the most.

**Pillar two — employer pension.** Funded by employer and employee contributions, and often the strongest pillar for expats because it starts the moment you start working here, however late you arrived. With the 30% ruling, build-up is generally based on taxable salary rather than the full package — check your pension statement.

**Pillar three — what you arrange yourself.** Savings, investments, annuities, property. This is the pillar that has to absorb an AOW gap.

## How the 2% rule actually works

SVB's wording leaves no room for interpretation: "Each year of insurance under the AOW scheme entitles you to 2% of a full AOW pension... You will get a full AOW pension if you have been insured for 50 years" (svb.nl, retrieved 20 August 2026), built up in the 50-year period before you reach AOW pension age.

Three details are easy to miss:

- **The window is the 50 years *before* your AOW age**, not your career.
- **Insurance follows residence and work, not employment status.** A year living here without working can still count, subject to the exceptions.
- **There is a floor.** Born on or after 1 January 1950 and insured for less than one year: no AOW pension at all.

Note what is *not* in this article: amounts. SVB publishes AOW rates itself, differentiated by living situation and revised twice a year, so check the AOW pension rates page on svb.nl for the figure that applies to your household.

## Are you insured? The general rule and the exceptions

SVB: "As a general rule, everyone who lives or works in the Netherlands is insured under the AOW scheme. However, there are exceptions."

Those exceptions matter to expats more than to anyone else. According to SVB you live here but are *not* insured if, among other situations, you work outside the Netherlands in the EU/EEA or a treaty country without a posting certificate, you work for a company outside the EU or a treaty country, you are a civil servant for an international organisation such as NATO or the EU, or you are studying here temporarily and not employed. Conversely, you can live *outside* the Netherlands and stay insured — for example if you are employed and pay Dutch tax and social insurance contributions without a posting certificate from another country.

If any of those describe a year in your history, ask SVB for a statement of insurance periods rather than assuming.

## The AOW gap, and what to do about it

For newcomers, SVB is explicit: "Your pension will be reduced by 2% for each year you were not living or working in the Netherlands. This means you will not receive a full AOW pension when you reach your AOW pension age" (svb.nl, retrieved 20 August 2026).

Work it out on the back of an envelope: count the insured years you expect before your AOW age, multiply by two, and you have your percentage. If that is well under 100, three responses are open to you, and they are not mutually exclusive.

**One: voluntary insurance.** SVB offers voluntary AOW insurance both to keep building up while you live or work abroad and to buy back past uninsured years. The buy-back route has conditions and deadlines and is priced on your income in the years concerned, so contact SVB early rather than at retirement.

**Two: pension rights you already hold elsewhere.** Many expats have preserved entitlements in a previous country. Whether to consolidate or leave them is a real decision with tax consequences in both countries — our guides to [transferring a Dutch pension](/blog/dutch-pension-transfer-expats-2026/) and [claiming a Dutch pension from abroad](/blog/claiming-dutch-pension-abroad-2026/) cover both directions.

**Three: pillar three, deliberately.** An expat with a 60% AOW entitlement has a structurally different retirement plan from a colleague with 100%. That is a reason to be intentional about long-term saving — and to know your actual net income today, which our [salary after tax calculator](/tools/salary-after-tax-calculator/) shows in a minute.

## Your AOW age is probably not 67

SVB publishes the AOW age by date of birth and flags which cohorts are final. As shown on svb.nl (page version 1 May 2026), ages are established for everyone born before 1 October 1964 — 67 years for births between 1 March 1957 and 31 December 1960, and 67 years and 3 months for births between 1 January 1961 and 30 September 1964. Later cohorts get *expected* ages, which rise in steps, because the AOW age tracks life expectancy and Statistics Netherlands revises its estimate every December.

If you are under 50, treat your AOW age as a moving target.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is the Dutch AOW state pension built up?

At 2% of a full AOW pension for each insured year, within the 50-year window before your AOW age; 50 insured years gives a full pension. Fewer than one insured year (born on or after 1 January 1950) gives none.

### Who counts as insured under the AOW scheme?

As a general rule everyone living or working in the Netherlands, with exceptions including posted workers holding a certificate from another country, staff of international organisations, and students temporarily here who are not employed.

### What is the AOW gap and do I have one?

If you arrived mid-career, your AOW is reduced by 2% for each year you were not living or working here — a partial AOW, not a nearly-full one.

### Can I buy back missing AOW years?

Sometimes, through voluntary insurance for past uninsured years. Conditions, deadlines and pricing vary, so ask SVB directly and early.

### At what age do I get AOW?

It depends on your date of birth. Ages are final only for people born before 1 October 1964; later cohorts have expected ages that shift with life expectancy.

### How do I find out how many AOW years I have?

Request a statement of insurance periods through My SVB with DigiD — the authoritative record, and easiest to correct early.
