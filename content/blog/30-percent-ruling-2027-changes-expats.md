The Dutch expat ruling — still universally called the 30% ruling, though the Belastingdienst now labels it the *expatregeling* — is changing in 2027. This is not a rumour or a line in a coalition document: it is published government policy with a transition table, and it lands on 1 January 2027.

What it is not is a single change that affects everyone equally. Whether you lose anything at all depends on when you started using the ruling, and much of the anxiety around this comes from people applying someone else's row of the table to their own situation.

> **Short answer:** Verified on 20 August 2026: rijksoverheid.nl states that the expat ruling's tax-free allowance stays at a maximum of 30% in 2025 and 2026 and is reduced to a maximum of **27% from 2027**, with a higher salary norm from 2027 for later entrants. The published transition table is clear: started **on or before 31-12-2023** → 30% and the current salary norm, also from 2027; started **during 2024** → 27% and the current salary norm from 2027; started **from 1-1-2025** → 27% and the new salary norm from 2027. The 2026 salary norm is €48,013, or €36,497 for under-30s with a Dutch academic master's, and the allowance applies to salary up to €262,000. Sources: [rijksoverheid.nl — expatregeling](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/inkomstenbelasting/belastingvoordeel-buitenlandse-werknemers) and [Belastingdienst — deskundigheidsvereiste](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/internationaal/personeel/u_bent_niet_in_nederland_gevestigd_loonheffingen_inhouden/als_u_loonheffingen_gaat_inhouden/extraterritoriale_kosten_en_de_30procentregeling/voorwaarden_voor_de_30procentregeling1/deskundigheidsvereiste).

## The transition table, and how to find your row

This is the part to get right before anything else. Rijksoverheid publishes the transition in three rows, based on when you first used the ruling:

| You started using the ruling | 2025–2026 | From 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| On or before 31-12-2023 | 30% and current salary norm | 30% and current salary norm |
| During 2024 | 30% and current salary norm | 27% and current salary norm |
| From 1-1-2025 | 30% and current salary norm | 27% and **new** salary norm |

Three observations worth stating plainly. The **pre-2024 group is protected on both dimensions**: if your ruling was already running by the end of 2023, the table keeps you at 30% with the current salary norm, including after 2027 — that is the group most likely to be worrying unnecessarily. The **2024 group loses the percentage but not the norm**, moving to 27% against the current salary norm. The **2025-onwards group is affected on both**.

The relevant date is when you *started using* the ruling, which follows the decision (*beschikking*) issued by the Belastingdienst — not when you moved here, and not when you signed a contract. Your beschikking and your payslips together tell you. For how the ruling behaves across a job change or a gap in employment, [the 30% ruling extension and 2024 changes article](/blog/30-percent-ruling-extension-2024-changes-2026/) covers the continuity conditions.

## The salary norm: what is known and what is not

The ruling has always had an income condition, indexed annually. For 2026 the Belastingdienst states a taxable annual salary above **€48,013**, with a reduced norm of **€36,497** for employees under 30 who obtained a Dutch master's degree in academic education. Researchers and medical specialists in training are exempt from the income norm.

From 2027, a higher salary norm applies to the group that entered from 1 January 2025. This is where we will not be more precise than the sources: the indexed 2027 amounts were not published at the verification date of 20 August 2026. They are confirmed through the yearly tax package and then appear on the Belastingdienst's own pages — a better source than any number circulating in expat forums in the autumn.

Practically: if your salary sits just above the current norm, do not assume you are safe in 2027 without checking the published figure when it arrives. Falling below the norm ends the ruling, which is a far bigger event than three percentage points.

## What the change costs: a labelled model calculation

The following is a **model calculation** for illustration only. It uses round numbers, ignores holiday allowance treatment, pension contributions, and any employer-specific arrangement, and it is not a prediction of your payslip.

The ruling allows a portion of your salary to be paid as a tax-free allowance for extraterritorial costs. Move that portion from 30% to 27% and three percentage points of salary shift from tax-free to taxable.

| Gross salary (model) | Tax-free at 30% | Tax-free at 27% | Difference in taxable income |
|---|---|---|---|
| €60,000 | €18,000 | €16,200 | €1,800 |
| €80,000 | €24,000 | €21,600 | €2,400 |
| €100,000 | €30,000 | €27,000 | €3,000 |
| €150,000 | €45,000 | €40,500 | €4,500 |

The *cash* effect is the shifted amount times your marginal rate. At a marginal rate around 49.5%, a €3,000 shift models out at roughly €1,485 a year; at a lower rate, less. Because the allowance applies to salary up to €262,000 — the Belastingdienst puts the 2026 maximum allowance at €78,600, i.e. 30% of the cap — earners above the cap feel it only up to that ceiling.

## What has not changed

The discourse around this ruling tends to imply the whole thing is being dismantled, so the stable parts are worth stating.

**The five-year duration.** Rijksoverheid still describes a maximum of five years of tax-free allowance under the conditions. The cut from eight years to five happened in an earlier reform, not this one.

**The basic structure.** It remains an employer-applied allowance for extraterritorial costs, requested from the Belastingdienst by employer and employee together and tied to qualifying employment.

**The alternative route.** Employers can still reimburse actual extraterritorial costs instead of using the percentage, which for people with genuinely high relocation and double-housing costs can produce a better outcome. Run that comparison with an adviser rather than assuming the percentage wins.

## What to do before January

**Identify your row in the table.** Everything else follows from it, and your beschikking date is the anchor.

**Ask your employer what they will apply.** Payroll teams handle this differently, and some large employers have already modelled 2027 for affected staff. Keep the question factual: which percentage from January, and against which salary norm.

**Check your headroom against the norm**, especially if you are near the threshold or a change in hours, part-time work or unpaid leave is on the horizon.

**Raise it in the salary conversation, if it applies to you.** Three points of tax-free allowance is a legitimate input to a normal review — not an entitlement to compensation, and framing it as one goes badly in a Dutch workplace. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the 30% ruling becoming 27%?

Yes, from 2027, per rijksoverheid.nl — but only for those not covered by the pre-2024 transition row. Employees who started using the ruling on or before 31 December 2023 keep 30% and the current salary norm.

### What is the salary threshold?

€48,013 for 2026, or €36,497 for under-30s with a Dutch academic master's. A higher norm applies from 2027 to those who started from 1 January 2025; the indexed 2027 amounts were not published on 20 August 2026.

### What is the cost of the change?

Three percentage points of salary move from tax-free to taxable, up to the €262,000 cap. The cash effect depends on your marginal rate.

### Does it still last five years?

Yes, five years under the conditions, per rijksoverheid.nl.

### Should I do anything before January?

Confirm which transition row applies to you, ask payroll what they will apply, and check your salary against the norm.

## My final thoughts

The pattern over the past few years has been steady narrowing: shorter duration, a salary cap, and now a lower percentage. Anyone planning a Dutch career on the assumption that the ruling looks the same in five years as it does today is planning on sand.

That said, the 2027 change is smaller than the noise around it. If your ruling was running before 2024, the published table leaves you where you are. If it started later, you lose three percentage points of tax-free allowance and possibly face a higher salary norm — meaningful, but not the cliff that forum threads suggest.

The line worth watching is the salary norm rather than the percentage. Three points of allowance is a manageable dent; falling below the income threshold ends the ruling entirely. We will update this article once the indexed 2027 norms are on belastingdienst.nl.
