> **Quick answer:** Moving house within the Netherlands as an expat means juggling rental notice (one calendar month), a gemeente address update within 5 days, energy and internet transfers, and roughly 50 small admin tasks. Start 4 weeks out, keep a single checklist (I use [verhuisplanner.app](https://verhuisplanner.app)), and submit meter readings on move day to avoid surprise bills.

I moved from Amsterdam-West to Utrecht-Oost last spring and I genuinely thought I had it sorted. I had already done the big international move from Brighton to Amsterdam four years earlier, so a domestic hop felt almost trivial. Two trains, one moving van, done.

It was not done.

Three weeks after the move a friend in my old building messaged me a photo of an envelope from the Belastingdienst. It was sitting on the communal mat, unopened, with my name on it. It turned out to be about my 30% ruling and required a response within fourteen days. I had updated my address with the gemeente, but I had not realised that the Belastingdienst sometimes still uses the address on file at the moment a letter is generated, and I had not flagged the move in Mijn Belastingdienst. I spent half a Saturday on hold and the rest of the weekend feeling like an idiot.

This guide is the checklist I wish I had used. It is written specifically for expats who already live in the Netherlands and are signing their second (or third) Dutch rental, not for people arriving from abroad. If you are moving into the country for the first time, start with my [complete moving to the Netherlands checklist](/blog/moving-to-netherlands-2027-complete-checklist/) instead.

## Four weeks before: the foundations

The single biggest mistake I see expats make is treating the move as a logistics problem (boxes, van, keys) when it is actually an admin problem. Boxes are easy. The admin tail is what eats your evenings for two months.

### Send your rental notice in writing

If you are on an indefinite contract (huurovereenkomst voor onbepaalde tijd), your notice period is one calendar month, ending on the last day of the month. Send notice by email AND aangetekende post (registered post). Keep proof. The cost of a registered letter at PostNL is around EUR 11 and it has saved me twice.

If you are on a fixed-term contract (bepaalde tijd, usually 12 or 24 months), check the cancellation clause carefully. Many fixed-term contracts cannot be ended early without the landlord's written agreement. A friend of mine paid two months of double rent because she did not read this clause.

Practical pointers:

- Notice must reach the landlord before the first of the month to count for that month.
- Some landlords ask for two months' notice in the contract. This is legally allowed.
- Always reference the contract date and your address in the notice email.
- Ask in the same email for the inspection date and the deposit refund timeline.

### Lock in the overlap

Try to negotiate two to four weeks of overlap between the old and new contract. Yes, you pay double rent for that period. Yes, it is worth it. You will need time to clean, paint, hand back keys, and recover. Trying to do a same-day handover with two landlords and a removal van in Dutch city traffic is the closest I have come to crying in public.

If you are still looking for the new place, [HousingAnywhere](/blog/finding-housing-netherlands-expats-2026/) and Pararius are the two platforms I would start with. Most landlord-side agents on Funda do not respond to expat enquiries, in my experience.

### Inspect the new place properly

Before you sign, walk through with the previous tenant or landlord and document everything on video. I am not exaggerating: open every cupboard, photograph every scratch on the floor, run the taps in both bathrooms, test the oven, check the boiler year sticker. Email the video to yourself the same day so it is timestamped.

A bad inspection report costs people their deposit. The Huurcommissie (rent tribunal) sees these disputes constantly.

### Understand the deposit handling

Dutch law (as of July 2023) caps rental deposits at two months' rent and requires landlords to return the deposit within 14 days of the contract ending, minus any agreed deductions. They must itemise deductions. If they do not respond within 30 days they owe you the full amount plus statutory interest.

I now refuse to pay a deposit in cash and I always transfer through a bank that gives me a clear transaction reference. If your current bank's app makes that messy, this is one of the reasons I switched to [bunq](/blog/dutch-bank-account-expats-2026/) two years ago.

## Three weeks before: the booking week

### Removal van or full-service movers?

You have roughly three options in the Netherlands:

**DIY with a rental van.** Bo-rent, Diks, and Hertz all rent vans. Expect EUR 80-150 per day for a Mercedes Sprinter sized vehicle, plus fuel, plus a kilometre charge above the included allowance. You will need a category B driver's licence and at least one strong friend who owes you a favour.

**Budget movers like Brenger or Mondu.** These are marketplace platforms that match you with independent drivers. Good for smaller moves (studio, one-bedroom). Prices start around EUR 150 for a few large items and EUR 400-600 for a full studio move. Booking is fully online in English.

**Full-service movers.** Companies like Mondial Movers, De Haan, and Voerman pack, transport, and assemble. Expect EUR 800-2,500 depending on volume and stairs. Worth it if you have heavy furniture, a piano, or you are moving with kids. See my breakdown in [best international moving companies in the Netherlands](/blog/best-international-moving-companies-netherlands-2026/) (the domestic services overlap heavily).

The hidden cost everyone forgets: **a verhuislift**. If your old or new building has no working lift and you are above the second floor, you will need an external furniture lift (verhuislift) parked on the street. Booking one through Riswick or a local supplier costs EUR 150-350 for half a day, and it needs to be booked at least two weeks ahead in major cities. The municipality usually requires a parking permit (parkeerontheffing) for the day, costing another EUR 30-60.

### Declutter ruthlessly

The cheapest box you move is the one you never pack. Two weekends before the move I now do a "three-pile sort" for every cupboard:

1. Keep
2. Sell on Marktplaats or Vinted
3. Donate to Kringloop or leave on the street with a "gratis" sign

Dutch second-hand culture is excellent. A pile of decent items on the pavement on a Saturday morning is usually gone by lunch. Just check your gemeente's bulky waste (grofvuil) rules so you do not get a fine for dumping.

### Order your boxes

Pretty obvious, but: order moving boxes a week before you need them, not the day before. Bol.com sells reusable plastic crates for rent. AH and Jumbo will sometimes give you free banana boxes if you ask the warehouse staff politely on a Tuesday morning. I know this because I have done it.

## Two weeks before: the utilities week

This is the section most expats underestimate. Utilities in the Netherlands do not magically transfer just because you have updated your gemeente address.

### Energy contract (gas and electricity)

You have two routes:

**Route 1: Cancel and start fresh.** Cancel your current contract effective on move day. Sign a new contract at the new address with a start date matching your key handover. Easiest if your current contract is open-ended or about to renew.

**Route 2: Take it with you (verhuisservice).** Most providers, including Vattenfall, Eneco, and Greenchoice, offer a free address change if you stay with them. Useful if you are mid-contract and would otherwise pay an early-termination fee (boete) of EUR 50-250.

Things to do regardless of route:

- Read both gas and electricity meters on move day. Photograph them with the date visible.
- If the new place has a smart meter (slimme meter), the reading is automatic, but I still take a photo for backup.
- Send the readings (meterstanden) to your provider within 5 working days.
- If you are moving into a place that was vacant, you may be on a "leveranciersloze periode" tariff for the first few days. This is fine and gets reconciled later.

Honestly, I would also use the move as an excuse to compare prices. I switched to a cheaper green provider during my Utrecht move and saved roughly EUR 28 per month. See my [Dutch energy providers comparison](/blog/dutch-energy-providers-comparison-2026/) for current rates.

### Internet

KPN, Ziggo, T-Mobile (now Odido), and Delta all offer a free verhuisservice. You typically need to give them 4 weeks notice so the engineer can be scheduled on the right day. Two things to watch:

- If the new address is not on your current provider's network (for example, your old flat had Ziggo coax but the new flat is fibre-only), you have the legal right to cancel without an early-termination fee. They will try to talk you out of this. Be polite but firm.
- Returning the old modem/router is your responsibility. Keep the PostNL return tracking code for two years. I cannot stress this enough.

My full breakdown sits at [Dutch internet providers for expats](/blog/dutch-internet-providers-expats-2026/).

### Water

This is the only utility you basically do not have to think about. Water is supplied by regional monopolies (Waternet in Amsterdam, Vitens in much of the rest of the country, Evides in Zeeland and South Holland). You do not switch providers. You just notify them of the move through their website, give a final meter reading, and they send a final bill. Setting up service at the new address takes about 90 seconds online.

### Waste collection (afvalstoffenheffing)

This is paid annually to your gemeente, not to a private company. When you register at the new address it gets added to your municipal taxes (gemeentelijke belastingen). The amount varies wildly: Amsterdam charges around EUR 360 per year for a single household, Utrecht around EUR 290, smaller gemeentes can be EUR 200 or less. You may receive a partial refund from your old gemeente if you paid for the full year upfront.

## One week before: the admin sprint

This is the part I now actively dread, which is why I started using a dedicated tool to track it (more on that in a moment).

### Update your address with the gemeente

This is the legal anchor. You must register the move (verhuizing doorgeven) with your new gemeente. The window is 4 weeks before to 5 days after the move date. After 5 days you risk a fine (up to EUR 325 in some gemeentes) and, more painfully, your government mail starts going to the wrong place.

You do this via DigiD on the gemeente website. For Amsterdam, that is [amsterdam.nl/burgerzaken/verhuizing](https://www.amsterdam.nl/burgerzaken/verhuizing/). Every gemeente has a similar URL. The form takes about 10 minutes.

If you are moving to a new gemeente (not just within the same city), you register in the new gemeente only. The new gemeente automatically de-registers you from the old one. Do not try to do both, it confuses the system.

### What updates automatically and what doesn't

Once the gemeente accepts your new address, the change flows automatically to:

- The Belastingdienst (mostly, but verify in Mijn Belastingdienst)
- UWV
- SVB
- DUO
- The IND (your residence permit address is updated)
- Your zorgverzekering insurer (sometimes — I would not rely on it)

You still have to update manually:

- Your bank (every single account)
- Your employer's HR system
- Your pension fund
- Private insurance (contents, liability, car, travel)
- Your GP (huisarts) and dentist
- Your kids' kinderopvang or school
- Your OV-chipkaart and NS Flex account
- Any subscriptions you receive physical post for
- Your DigiD profile (yes, even though it logged you in to do all this, the postal address is separate)

For DigiD specifically, the postal address gets updated automatically from BRP, but if you have set a different correspondence address you need to change it. My [DigiD guide](/blog/digid-netherlands-expats-2026/) walks through this. Your [BSN](/blog/bsn-number-netherlands-guide-2026/) itself does not change, only the address linked to it.

For banks, the process varies. ING and Rabobank pick up the BRP change within a week. ABN AMRO usually wants you to confirm in the app. Revolut, Wise, and bunq do not get the BRP feed and must be updated manually in-app.

### Health and family admin

Notify your huisarts. If you are moving to a different city, you usually need to register with a new practice, and good practices in the Randstad have waiting lists of 6-12 months. Start this conversation early. Ask your current practice to send your medical file to the new one (they will not do it automatically).

If you have a baseline health insurance (basisverzekering), the move itself does not affect coverage. If you have travel-related coverage like [SafetyWing](/blog/aanvullende-supplemental-health-insurance-expats-2026/), update the home address there too so claims are processed against the right country.

If you have kids in kinderopvang or school: notify both the current and new provider. School registration in a new gemeente can take weeks and there may be waiting lists. Toeslagen for childcare (kinderopvangtoeslag) need a separate address update in Mijn Toeslagen.

### Belastingdienst (do not skip this)

This is where I tripped up. Even though the BRP feeds the Belastingdienst, there is a delay of 1-4 weeks. During that window letters can still be generated to the old address. I now do two things on move day:

1. Log into Mijn Belastingdienst and verify the new address on the personal details screen.
2. Set up a forwarding service (PostNL Verhuisservice) for 3 months as a safety net. It costs EUR 21.99 and forwards all post to the new address. Worth every cent.

Also: if you have the 30% ruling, log a note in your file (or email your employer's tax adviser) that you have moved. The ruling itself is not address-dependent but employers sometimes need the updated address for the annual review.

## Tracking all 50+ tasks: verhuisplanner.app

I tried to manage my first Dutch domestic move with a Notion page and a Google Doc. By week three I had three overlapping versions of the same list and I still missed the Belastingdienst notification.

For the Utrecht move I tested [verhuisplanner.app](https://verhuisplanner.app). It is a Dutch-built planning tool specifically designed for moves within (or to) the Netherlands. You enter your move date and it generates a timeline of every task: rental notice, gemeente, energy, internet, water, banks, employer, insurance, OV-chipkaart, schools, deposit follow-up. Each task has a due date relative to your move and you tick them off as you go.

What I found genuinely useful:

- It includes the legal deadlines (5 days for gemeente, 14 days for deposit refund, etc.) so you actually know which ones can cost you money if you miss them.
- It separates "automatic via BRP" from "manual" tasks, which is exactly the thing I got wrong the first time.
- The export to calendar function dropped everything into my Google Calendar with the right reminders.

If you only take one thing from this article, it is this: do not try to track a Dutch move in your head or in a generic to-do app. The combination of legal deadlines, automatic-but-not-quite government updates, and manual provider notifications is genuinely too much to hold. ## Move week: the logistics

### The day before

- Defrost the freezer (it takes longer than you think)
- Pack a "first night box": kettle, mugs, tea, toilet paper, phone charger, sheets, a bottle of wine, painkillers
- Confirm the verhuislift booking and check the weather forecast
- Charge a power bank
- Print or screenshot the new building's house rules and lift booking confirmation

### Move day

- Read all meters at the old place. Photograph.
- Read all meters at the new place. Photograph.
- Walk through the old place with the landlord or agent. Use your inspection video from earlier.
- Hand over keys. Ask for written confirmation that the inspection was satisfactory.
- At the new place, check the boiler is on, the wifi works (most providers' verhuisservice activates the line at midnight on move day), and the smoke detectors have batteries.

### The first week

- Walk to your new gemeente's neighbourhood waste container and figure out where the glass, paper, and general waste go. Every Dutch street has its own rhythm.
- Introduce yourself to immediate neighbours. In the Netherlands this is genuinely expected and it makes future "your bike is blocking my door" conversations much easier.
- Find your nearest huisarts, supermarket, and AH-to-go.
- Update your address on your OV-chipkaart via the NS website (so the season ticket maps to the right station if you have one).

## What it actually costs (my numbers)

For full transparency, here is what my Amsterdam-to-Utrecht move cost in spring 2026:

- Brenger movers (1-bedroom, no piano): EUR 485
- Verhuislift in Amsterdam (half day): EUR 220
- Parking permit (Amsterdam): EUR 48
- Boxes and tape: EUR 65
- Cleaning service for the old flat: EUR 180
- PostNL Verhuisservice (3 months): EUR 22
- Double rent (2 weeks overlap): EUR 875
- New gemeente waste tax (pro-rata): EUR 110
- Lost productivity (estimated): a lot

Total out-of-pocket: EUR 2,005. The deposit on the old flat came back in full after 11 days, which softened the blow, but I would budget around EUR 2,000-2,500 for a typical one-bedroom move within the same region. A two-bedroom move with full-service movers easily reaches EUR 4,000-5,000.

## What I would do differently next time

Three things, honestly.

First, I would start the gemeente process the same week I signed the new contract, not the week before the move. The system lets you submit up to 4 weeks ahead with a future move date and it removes the entire "did the BRP update yet" anxiety.

Second, I would use a dedicated tracker from day one. I wasted maybe 12 hours over the two-month window because I was rebuilding the same list in different apps. [verhuisplanner.app](https://verhuisplanner.app) or any equivalent solves this.

Third, I would book the cleaning service before I booked the movers. Good end-of-tenancy cleaners in the Randstad are booked out 3-4 weeks ahead. Bad cleaners cost you part of your deposit. The maths is obvious in hindsight.

## Final thought

Moving house within the Netherlands is not hard. It is just relentlessly detailed. The country runs on tidy administration, and that administration assumes that you are paying attention. If you build the right checklist on day one, follow it, and submit your meter readings on move day, you will be fine. If you try to wing it, you will spend a rainy Saturday on hold with the Belastingdienst, like I did.

Be kind to yourself in the two weeks after the move. You will forget where you put the kettle. The first time you cook in a new kitchen is always slightly bad. And the day you find the last unopened box at the back of a cupboard is the day you have truly settled in.

Good luck with the move. Doei.
