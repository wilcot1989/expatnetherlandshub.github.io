"Unlimited" is the most misread word in Dutch telecom advertising. It is not a lie — the domestic data volume really is uncapped — but the limit has simply moved somewhere less obvious. In the Netherlands, unlimited plans are sold in **speed tiers**, and the cheapest unlimited plan on the market can be slower than a mid-range bundled plan.

That matters more for expats than for locals, because a lot of new arrivals buy unlimited data as an insurance policy: no home internet yet, no idea what normal usage looks like here, no appetite for a nasty overage bill in month one. Reasonable instinct — but it can mean paying network-operator prices for a connection capped at a fraction of network speed.

## What Dutch providers mean by "unlimited"

Three things are being limited even on an unlimited plan.

**Speed.** This is the main lever. Providers sell the same uncapped volume at different maximum throughputs, and the entry tier is deliberately modest. Upgrading a tier typically costs a few euros a month.

**EU roaming volume.** Roam-like-at-home rules explicitly allow a fair-use policy for roaming, and every unlimited plan converts to a fixed gigabyte allowance the moment you cross a border. Published caps vary a great deal by provider — from the low tens of gigabytes up to around 75 GB.

**Fair use in general.** Contract terms allow providers to act against use that is not consistent with a personal mobile subscription: running a business connection off it, permanent roaming from another country, or replacing a fixed line for an entire household.

Nothing here is unusual or hidden — it is all in the terms — but it does mean that comparing unlimited plans on headline price alone compares almost nothing.

Introductory promotions sit on top of all of this. It is normal for a Dutch two-year plan to run at half price or less for the first 6–12 months. That is real money — but the figure that determines what you actually spend across 24 months is the standard rate you revert to, so read both.

## EU roaming: where unlimited stops being unlimited

If you travel within Europe regularly — weekend trips, family visits, cross-border work — the roaming allowance may matter more to you than the domestic speed tier.

Published EU allowances on Dutch unlimited plans currently span a wide band, from around 20 GB at the budget end to roughly 75 GB on some operator plans, with providers sitting at different points in between and occasionally listing different figures across their own pages and comparison sites. Past the allowance you pay per gigabyte at the regulated surcharge.

Practical guidance: find the number in your own plan's terms before a long trip, and if you spend several weeks a year abroad, weigh the roaming cap alongside the price. Someone spending one week a month in Germany can easily be better off on a slightly more expensive plan with triple the roaming allowance.

Also remember the UK sits outside the EU roaming rules entirely, and Dutch providers price UK roaming separately. Switzerland is likewise outside the EU/EEA scheme, though some plans include it voluntarily.

## Does anyone actually need unlimited?

Here is the more useful question: how much data do you use in a month?

If you have never checked, both iOS and Android track it. Look at the last three months before you buy anything. The typical pattern for someone with Wi-Fi at home and at work is well under 20 GB — the phone spends most of its waking hours on a fixed connection.

**Unlimited is worth it when:**

- You tether a laptop regularly — the single biggest consumer of mobile data.
- You have no fixed home internet, by choice or because the connection has not been installed yet.
- You commute on Dutch trains daily and stream video rather than music.
- You are on call for work and cannot risk a throttled connection at the end of a bundle.
- The peace of mind is worth €8–€10 a month to you. That is a legitimate reason and there is no need to over-analyse it.

**A 15–30 GB bundle is the better buy when:**

- You have solid home and office Wi-Fi.
- Your out-of-home usage is maps, messaging, music and social media.
- You want a one-month cancellable plan, which is more common and cheaper in the bundled tiers.

The price gap is not trivial. Bundled sim-only plans in the 10–30 GB range routinely sit in the €5–€15 band at standard rates from budget brands — roughly half of unlimited, sometimes less. Over two years that difference is a few hundred euros for capacity many people never touch.

## The home internet question

New arrivals waiting on a fibre installation often ask whether an unlimited mobile plan can replace home broadband. The honest answer is: as a stopgap, sometimes; as a permanent arrangement, rarely.

The constraint is the speed tier, not the volume. An unlimited plan capped around 20–25 Mbit/s will serve one person browsing, working on cloud documents and streaming HD comfortably. It will not serve a household with two people on video calls, a TV streaming 4K and a games console downloading a 90 GB update. Tethering also runs your phone's battery down hard and puts household traffic on a connection that drops when you leave the building.

If you are bridging a few weeks before installation, tethering an unlimited plan is a perfectly sensible move. ## What you need before you can buy one

Unlimited plans are postpaid subscriptions, which means the two standard Dutch requirements apply:

- **A BSN.** Providers run a credit check tied to it. See our [BSN application guide](/blog/expat-bsn-application-guide-2026/).
- **A Dutch IBAN** for the monthly direct debit. Our [step-by-step bank account guide](/blog/getting-a-dutch-bank-account-step-by-step-2026/) covers the fastest routes.

If you have neither yet, you are on prepaid for now — and no prepaid product in the Netherlands offers unlimited data at a price that makes sense. Start with the [prepaid comparison](/blog/best-prepaid-sim-card-netherlands-2026/), then revisit unlimited once your paperwork is done.

Be aware that handset-included plans are treated differently from sim-only: financing a phone through a Dutch provider is a credit agreement and, above the applicable threshold, is registered with BKR. Sim-only plans are the clean option if you want to keep your credit file simple during your first years here.

## Contract length: the decision people get wrong

Dutch unlimited plans are cheapest on 24-month terms and most flexible on monthly ones, with a price difference of a few euros a month.

For expats the calculation is different from the one a Dutch household makes. If there is any realistic chance you leave the country, change employer to one that provides a phone, or move in with a partner whose plan you can share, the monthly-cancellable option at a slightly higher price is worth the premium. Leaving a two-year contract early normally means paying out the remaining term, and moving abroad is not a get-out clause.

If you are certain about staying — permanent contract, partner, house — take the two-year term and the promotional pricing.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does an unlimited data plan cost in the Netherlands in 2026?

Standard rates broadly run €20–€31 per month depending on provider and speed tier. Introductory promotions can halve the first months of a two-year term, so check what you pay after the promotion ends.

### Is unlimited data in the Netherlands really unlimited?

The domestic volume is uncapped, but the speed is tiered. The cheapest unlimited plans can be limited to around 20–25 Mbit/s, with faster tiers available for a few euros more.

### Does unlimited data work when I travel in the EU?

Yes, but with a fair-use cap. Published EU roaming allowances on Dutch unlimited plans range from roughly 20 GB to around 75 GB per month, after which a per-gigabyte surcharge applies.

### Do I need unlimited data, or is 30 GB enough?

For most people with home and office Wi-Fi, 15–30 GB is comfortable and costs roughly half as much. Unlimited pays off if you tether a laptop, have no home internet, or stream video daily on the move.

### Can I use an unlimited plan as home internet?

As a temporary bridge, often yes. As a permanent household connection, the speed tier and the fair-use terms usually make a proper broadband contract the better answer.

### Do I need a BSN to get an unlimited plan?

Yes. Unlimited plans are postpaid, and Dutch providers require a BSN for the credit check plus a Dutch IBAN for direct debit.

### What happens to my unlimited plan if I leave the Netherlands?

A fixed term does not end because you move away — you normally pay out the remaining months. If your stay has a foreseeable end date, choose a monthly cancellable plan.

## The short version

Unlimited data in the Netherlands is a speed decision dressed up as a volume decision. Work out what you actually use, check whether you tether, look at the roaming allowance if you travel, and only then compare prices — including the price after the promotion expires.

For a lot of expats the honest conclusion is that unlimited is a comfort purchase rather than a necessity, and a 30 GB bundle at half the price would never once have run out. For the minority who work from trains and tether laptops, it is the cheapest way to stop thinking about data entirely.
