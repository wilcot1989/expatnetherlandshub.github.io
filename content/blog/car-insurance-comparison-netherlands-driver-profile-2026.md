## The Three Questions That Decide It

Before the profiles, the framework. Every sensible coverage decision in the Dutch market answers three questions in this order.

**1. What would this car cost to replace on the Dutch market today?**

Not what you paid. Not what it is worth in Germany or the UK. What a comparable car with comparable mileage sells for here, now. Dutch used-car prices vary sharply by fuel type: median asking prices in mid-2026 sat around EUR 11,990 for petrol and EUR 9,497 for diesel, against roughly EUR 29,780 for hybrids and EUR 31,900 for electric cars, according to autokopen.nl's market data. A petrol hatchback and an electric crossover are not the same insurance problem even if both are five years old.

**2. If that amount vanished tomorrow, is it a problem or an annoyance?**

Casco cover (the WA+ and allrisk part) is not a value calculation, it is a volatility calculation. Insuring a EUR 4,000 car against total loss when you have savings is a poor trade — you are paying a premium plus an excess to protect an amount you could absorb. Insuring a EUR 25,000 car without casco is the opposite mistake.

**3. Is anyone else entitled to a say?**

A finance agreement, a lease contract or an employer arrangement can require a specific level of cover. That obligation overrides your own arithmetic. Check the contract before comparing prices.

## Reading the 2026 Averages Correctly

Geld.nl's 2026 comparison monitor reports average monthly premiums of EUR 109.57 for WA, EUR 100.17 for WA+ and EUR 111.49 for allrisk in the first half of 2026, after increases of 11.6%, 17.9% and 12.2% respectively over 2025.

The WA average sitting *above* the WA+ average confuses people, so it is worth being explicit: those are averages of different populations, not prices for the same car. WA-only policies cluster among older vehicles, younger drivers and higher-risk postcodes. WA+ and allrisk are bought disproportionately by people with newer cars and long claim-free records who receive large discounts. For any single car and driver, WA is cheaper than WA+, which is cheaper than allrisk.

What the averages *are* useful for is calibration. If your quote is far above them and your profile is ordinary, something in your file is working against you — often unrecognised foreign claim-free years. And because premiums rose 12–18% during 2026, a renewal offer computed off last year's book is not evidence of a competitive price.

At the very cheap end of the market, autoverzekering.nl reported monthly premiums starting from EUR 12.64 for WA, EUR 14.47 for WA+ and EUR 18.95 for allrisk in August 2026, with FBTO the cheapest in their comparison. Treat those as the floor for an ideal profile — an older, light, low-mileage car with a driver who has maximum claim-free years — not as a price you should expect.

## Profile 1: New or Nearly New Car (Under Five Years, Over EUR 15,000)

**Default: allrisk.**

This is the one profile where the answer is close to automatic. The gap between allrisk and WA+ on a modern car is usually a few tens of euros a month; the gap in exposure is the entire value of the vehicle for any damage that is your fault or unattributable — the car-park scrape, the bollard, the multi-car pile-up where liability is contested for eight months.

Three details matter more than the headline premium:

- **The valuation regime.** Dutch casco policies typically reimburse the *nieuwwaarde* (purchase value) only for an initial period, then apply a depreciation schedule, then fall back to *dagwaarde* — market value at the moment of loss. The length of those first two windows differs between insurers and is one of the few clauses that genuinely changes what you own. Compare it directly.
- **The excess.** A higher voluntary eigen risico lowers the premium. Set it at a figure you could pay tomorrow without borrowing.
- **Repair network.** Contracted-garage policies are usually faster and sometimes cheaper. Confirm there is one within a sane distance and whether original parts are guaranteed.

## Profile 2: Older Car (Roughly EUR 3,000–8,000, Seven Years or More)

**Default: WA, with WA+ as a considered upgrade.**

Cars of 7 to 12 years old had a median asking price around EUR 10,995 in mid-2026, and 12 to 17 years old around EUR 5,000. Once a car is in that lower band, full casco cover starts to cost a visible fraction of the car's value every year, and the payout is capped at dagwaarde anyway — a figure that keeps falling while the premium does not.

The case for stepping up to WA+ on an older car is not about collisions. It is about the named perils that write off a cheap car outright:

- **Theft**, which is concentrated in specific cities and specific models.
- **Fire.**
- **Storm and hail damage**, which is a real and repeated event in the Netherlands and can total a low-value car on bodywork costs alone.
- **Glass**, where a windscreen replacement can be a meaningful share of a EUR 4,000 car's value.

If you park on the street in a city, WA+ is often the better buy at this value level. If you park off-street in a quiet area and could replace the car from savings, WA is the honest answer and the difference is money you keep.

What not to do: buy allrisk on a EUR 5,000 car because it feels safer. You are paying comprehensive prices for a dagwaarde settlement.

## Profile 3: Imported Car

**Default: as per value — but settle the valuation question before you buy casco.**

The coverage logic does not change when you import. What changes is the number your claim will be settled against. Casco claims are paid on Dutch market value, and for a model with a thin local market — an unusual trim, a right-hand-drive car, a variant never sold here in volume — that value can be lower and more disputable than the price you paid abroad.

Practical steps if your car came with you:

1. Ask prospective insurers, before buying, how they establish dagwaarde for your specific model, and whether they use a recognised price list (koerslijst) or an independent valuation.
2. Keep the import file: purchase invoice, the BPM declaration, the RDW registration, and any valuation report used for import. It is the same evidence base a claims adjuster will want.
3. Reconsider casco honestly. If the Dutch market value is well below what the car is worth to you, no policy will bridge that gap, and paying allrisk premiums will not change the settlement basis.

The import process itself — RDW inspection, BPM, plates, when road tax starts — is covered in [importing your car into the Netherlands](/blog/importing-your-car-into-netherlands-bpm-2026/).

## Profile 4: Lease Driver

**Default: buy nothing extra until you have read the lease's insurance annex.**

A private lease in the Netherlands normally bundles insurance into the monthly price, typically at WA+ level, with allrisk included or offered as an upgrade on newer and electric models. Buying a second standalone policy for the same vehicle is not a coverage improvement; it is a duplicate.

What actually deserves your attention as a lease driver:

- **The excess** on the bundled policy, and whether an excess-reduction option is available.
- **The end-of-contract damage assessment**, which is where lease drivers get surprised. Lessors apply fair wear-and-tear standards, and kerbed wheels, deep scratches and interior damage attract charges. Photograph every panel, wheel and interior surface at delivery.
- **What is not bundled**: mileage overruns, and damage arising while you were not validly licensed.

Details on lease products, providers and contract terms are in [car leasing options for expats](/blog/car-leasing-options-expats-netherlands-2026/), and the ownership-versus-lease money question is in [leasing vs buying a car as an expat](/blog/leasing-vs-buying-car-expat-netherlands-2026/).

## Profile 5: Young Driver (Under 25) and New Arrivals With No Recognised History

**Default: the cover your car's value justifies — but fix the discount problem first.**

Age and claim-free years are among the strongest premium factors in the Dutch market, which puts drivers under 25 and freshly arrived expats in a similar position: a base premium with little or no ladder discount applied.

The instinct is to cut cover. That is the least effective lever available to you. Two better ones:

**Get foreign claim-free years recognised.** Request a formal no-claims statement (schadevrije jaren verklaring) from your previous insurer, in English or Dutch, and ask each Dutch insurer *in writing* how many steps they will credit. Insurers differ substantially, and the difference between step 0 and step 5 is a large share of the premium — far more than the WA+/allrisk gap.

**Use the excess and the mileage band deliberately.** A realistically low annual mileage and a voluntary excess you can genuinely afford both reduce the premium without reducing what is covered.

Only after both of those should you revisit the coverage level, and then on value grounds alone.

---

## Profile 6: Second Car, Low Mileage, or a Car You Barely Use

**Default: WA or WA+, with the mileage band checked annually.**

A car doing 5,000 kilometres a year is a different risk from one doing 25,000, and Dutch insurers price mileage bands explicitly. If your driving pattern changed — you moved closer to work, switched to the train, started cycling — tell your insurer. Overstating mileage is a quiet, recurring overpayment.

For context on what a car costs in total when it is barely used: Nibud calculates average monthly costs for a petrol car of EUR 370 for the mini class, EUR 432 for the compact class, EUR 572 for the small mid class and EUR 724 for the mid class, on assumptions of ten years' ownership and 8,500–11,000 kilometres a year. Insurance is a minority of that figure; depreciation is the largest line. If the car is genuinely marginal, the coverage question may be less relevant than whether to keep it at all.

## The Comparison Method, Per Profile

Whatever profile you fall into, the buying process is the same four moves.

1. **Fix the coverage level first, on value grounds.** Do not let a price list choose it for you.
2. **Compare the whole market in one pass.** Direct quotes tell you what one insurer thinks. A comparison run covers the mainstream market and shows whether your number is normal or an outlier.
3. **Compare the clauses, not only the premium.** Valuation regime, excess, glass cover, repair network, European cover, notice period.
4. **Re-run it at renewal.** Dutch insurers price new customers more aggressively than existing ones, and 2026's increases were not applied evenly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I decide between WA, WA+ and allrisk?**

Start from current Dutch market value: allrisk above roughly EUR 15,000 or under five years old, WA+ between roughly EUR 5,000 and EUR 15,000, WA below that. Then override for your profile — lease, import, finance obligation, or a total loss you could absorb from savings.

**What is the average car insurance price in the Netherlands in 2026?**

Geld.nl's 2026 monitor: EUR 109.57 for WA, EUR 100.17 for WA+ and EUR 111.49 for allrisk per month in the first half of 2026, after 11.6–17.9% increases over 2025. Those averages mix all driver profiles.

**Why is WA more expensive than WA+ in those averages?**

Different populations, not different prices for one car. WA-only is concentrated among higher-risk profiles; WA+ and allrisk among drivers with large claim-free discounts.

**Does allrisk pay what I paid for the car?**

Typically only during an initial purchase-value window, then via a depreciation schedule, then at dagwaarde. Check how long those windows last — it is the clause that determines what allrisk is worth on a two-year-old car.

**Do I need extra insurance on a private lease?**

Normally no. The lease bundles cover, usually WA+. Focus on the excess and the end-of-contract damage schedule instead.

**Does an imported car need different cover?**

The same coverage logic applies, but ask how the insurer establishes Dutch market value for your model before buying casco, and keep the import and valuation documents.

**I have no Dutch claim-free years. Should I take less cover?**

No. Get your foreign claim-free years formally recognised first — that usually moves the premium more than dropping a coverage level, and without adding risk.

## Final Thoughts

The three Dutch coverage levels are not a quality ladder where more is better. They are three different answers to the question of who absorbs the loss of your car, and the right answer changes with the car's Dutch market value, your ability to absorb that loss, and any contract that has a claim on the decision.

Get the level right on value grounds, then get the price right by comparing the whole market with your claim-free years counted. Doing it in that order is the difference between cheap insurance and insurance that is worth having.
