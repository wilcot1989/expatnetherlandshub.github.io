Every January the same message arrives from readers in their first Dutch winter: *my energy bill is nothing like what I was told it would be*. They are rarely wrong about the number — they were simply never shown how a Dutch energy bill is put together.

Dutch energy billing has one design feature that catches almost every newcomer. You do not pay for what you used last month. You pay a fixed monthly advance based on an estimate, and the truth arrives once a year in a single document. If that estimate was set too low when you moved in, the first winter is where the gap becomes visible.

> **Short answer:** Your monthly payment (termijnbedrag) is an advance based on an estimate, not a bill for what you used. Once a year the jaarafrekening compares estimate with reality and settles the difference — so an under-estimated monthly amount plus a first cold winter equals a large one-off bill. Energy tax adds a fixed amount per unit: in 2026, €0.11085 per kWh and €0.7268 per m3 including VAT, against a fixed annual reduction of €629 including VAT on your electricity connection (Belastingdienst tariff table and rijksoverheid.nl, retrieved 20 August 2026). The fix is to check your termijnbedrag in October, not in February.

## The three numbers on your bill, and which one you control

Every Dutch energy bill is built from the same components, and it helps to know which are negotiable.

**Commodity — the energy itself.** Price per kWh and per m³, set by your supplier and contract type. The part you influence by switching or using less.

**Network charges (netbeheerkosten).** Paid to your regional grid operator, which you cannot choose — it follows your address.

**Taxes.** Energy tax per unit plus VAT, offset by a fixed annual reduction. Identical for every supplier.

Only the first is competitive: when a comparison site shows a difference between offers, it lives in the commodity price and the standing charge, not in the taxes.

## What the tax actually adds

For 2026, the first-bracket energy tax figures are:

- **Electricity:** €0.09161 per kWh excluding VAT — **€0.11085 per kWh including VAT**
- **Gas:** €0.60066 per m³ excluding VAT — **€0.7268 per m³ including VAT**

(Source: Belastingdienst tariff table for environmental taxes, retrieved 20 August 2026.)

Against those sits the **belastingvermindering**: a fixed annual reduction on the energy tax for your electricity connection, **€629 including VAT in 2026** (rijksoverheid.nl, energiebelasting, retrieved 20 August 2026). It is a flat credit, so it softens a small bill proportionally more than a large one.

Now apply that to a cold month. Every additional cubic metre of gas you burn to heat a poorly insulated Dutch terraced house costs the commodity price *plus* €0.7268 in tax. That is why the difference between a mild December and a cold one is not marginal, and why insulation improvements pay back faster than the commodity price alone suggests.

## Why the first winter specifically

Four things stack up in your first year, all avoidable once you know about them.

**One: the estimate was made without you.** At sign-up the supplier has no consumption history for you in that home, so it uses a household profile or the property's past usage. Move in during summer and the estimate leans on months with almost no heating demand.

**Two: your monthly amount was never corrected.** Nothing forces a mid-year adjustment; the system will let you underpay for eleven months and settle in one document.

**Three: Dutch housing is heat-hungry in ways that surprise people.** Single glazing in older rentals, unheated hallways, floor-level draughts, and boilers still running the previous tenant's schedule.

**Four: heating habits transfer badly.** Arrivals from warmer countries often heat continuously because that works at home; arrivals from colder countries assume Dutch insulation matches theirs. Both patterns cost money here.

## The October checklist

This is the part worth acting on, and it takes about twenty minutes.

1. **Find your termijnbedrag in your supplier's portal** and note the annual consumption in kWh and m³ it assumes.
2. **Compare that assumption with reality** — the consumption on your last jaarafrekening, or your current meter readings against the move-in reading.
3. **Adjust the monthly amount upward if the assumption is low.** Spreading the difference over the winter costs you nothing overall; the annual total is the same either way.
4. **Record and photograph your meter readings**, with a visible date, on move-in and move-out without exception.
5. **Check your thermostat schedule.** Setting back overnight and while the home is empty is the cheapest change available to a renter.
6. **Read your contract's end date and exit terms** before you consider switching.

## Should you switch supplier before the cold months?

Sometimes, and autumn is a reasonable moment to look, because a switch resets your monthly amount at the same time.

Compare on the things that actually differ: the contract type — fixed, variable, or dynamic hourly pricing — the contract term, the standing charge, whether a welcome discount is flattering the first-year figure, and the early-exit fee. Then run the comparison using *your own* annual kWh and m³ from your last statement, not a default profile for "a couple in an apartment". Household profiles are exactly what produce mis-set monthly amounts.

## What I would not do

**Do not treat a lowered termijnbedrag as a saving.** Reducing the monthly payment does not reduce consumption; it relocates the cost to the settlement.

**Do not ignore the jaarafrekening.** Errors after a move — wrong meter, wrong start date, missing reading — are common and easiest to fix in the first weeks after it arrives.

**Do not judge a rental's energy use by its rent.** If you are still house-hunting, ask for the energy label and last year's consumption. That answer tells you more about your winter budget than the rent does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a termijnbedrag and why is mine wrong?

A fixed monthly advance based on estimated annual consumption, not a bill. Estimates made when you moved in are often too low for a newcomer, and nothing corrects them automatically. Adjust it in your supplier's portal.

### What is the jaarafrekening and when does it arrive?

The annual settlement comparing actual consumption with what you paid, arriving on your contract or meter-reading anniversary rather than in January.

### How much of my energy bill is tax?

In 2026, €0.11085 per kWh and €0.7268 per m³ including VAT in the first bracket, offset by a fixed reduction of €629 including VAT on your electricity connection. Identical for every supplier.

### Why does my bill jump so much in winter?

Heating dominates consumption, Dutch housing varies widely in insulation, and each extra cubic metre carries the fixed tax as well as the commodity price.

### Should I submit my own meter readings?

With a traditional meter, yes, and photograph it. With a smart meter, still record move-in and move-out readings — that is where disputes start.

### Is it worth switching supplier before winter?

Possibly, if you compare like-for-like on contract type, term, standing charge, discounts and exit fees, using your own annual consumption rather than a default profile.

## Final thoughts

Nothing about Dutch energy billing is designed to catch you out, but nothing warns you either. The monthly amount looks like a bill, behaves like a subscription, and settles like a tax return.

Spend twenty minutes in your supplier's portal this month: check the consumption your monthly amount assumes, correct it if it is fantasy, and photograph your meter. That is the whole intervention.
