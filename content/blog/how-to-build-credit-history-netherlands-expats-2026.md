Expats who arrive from the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada or India tend to ask the same question within their first few months: how do I start building credit here? It is a reasonable question with an awkward answer. The Netherlands does not have a consumer credit score. There is nothing to build in the sense that most newcomers mean it — no number that rises when you use a credit card responsibly, no rating agency selling you a monthly reading of your own reliability.

What the country has instead is a factual credit register, a set of affordability rules that lean heavily on your payslip, and a handful of institutions that will judge you on documents rather than on a rating. Understanding that difference saves you from the two mistakes newcomers make most often: taking on credit you do not need in the belief that it will help you later, and assuming your excellent home-country file will count for something at a Dutch mortgage desk.

> **Quick answer:** There is no Dutch credit score. Credit agreements above €250 running longer than a month are recorded at BKR in Tiel; a plain entry is neutral and an arrears code is the thing that hurts. Instead of chasing a score, build a documented Dutch track record: registered address, a Dutch bank account in your own name, stable declared income, direct debits that never bounce, and a BKR file you have checked yourself for free at mijnkredietregistratie.nl.

## Why the Dutch system works differently

In an Anglo-Saxon credit system, lenders sell risk to each other and price it using a score. Dutch consumer lending grew up under a different premise: consumer credit is something the state would rather you did not need. The register exists mainly to prevent over-indebtedness, not to help banks upsell.

That has three practical consequences for you.

**First, absence of credit is not suspicious.** In the US, a "thin file" is a problem in itself. In the Netherlands, having no credit agreements at all is the default condition of a financially healthy adult. Nobody at ABN AMRO will wonder why a 34-year-old software engineer has never had a car loan.

**Second, credit reduces rather than builds your capacity.** Open credit — a revolving facility, a leased phone handset, a credit card with a repayment option — is counted against you when a mortgage lender calculates what you can borrow, because the rules assume a monthly burden on the outstanding limit rather than on your actual balance. Opening a credit line "to build history" can measurably lower the house you can buy.

**Third, your income document is the score.** A Dutch permanent employment contract with a stated gross annual salary is worth more in a lending conversation than any behavioural record. This is why expats with the same salary get very different answers depending on whether their contract is permanent, temporary, or a freelance arrangement.

## What BKR actually is

Stichting BKR is a private foundation in Tiel that has operated the central credit information system since 1965. Every licensed credit provider in the Netherlands is a member and is obliged to report to it and to consult it before granting credit.

Credit providers register a credit agreement when it is **more than €250 and runs for longer than one month**. That includes:

- personal loans (persoonlijke lening) and revolving credit (doorlopend krediet)
- credit cards with a repayment facility, and overdraft facilities on a current account above the threshold
- buy-now-pay-later and instalment purchases above the threshold
- the financed handset portion of a mobile phone contract, when that portion exceeds €250 — the calling and data element is never registered
- student debt is handled separately by DUO and is not a BKR entry, though mortgage lenders ask about it directly

What is **not** in your BKR file: your salary, your rent, your utility bills, your health insurance premium, your current account balance, or your tax position. Missing a rent payment does not create a BKR entry. Persistent non-payment can still reach you through a debt collector and, in serious cases, court, but the register is specifically about credit.

### Codes, and which ones matter

A normal entry is descriptive: type of credit, amount, start date, expected end date. If you fall behind, the provider may report an arrears code — the well-known A-code — typically once you are between two and four months late. Alongside that sit a small set of special codes for situations such as a payment arrangement, an amount written off, or a credit that has become unrecoverable.

The distinction matters enormously and is where most English-language forum advice goes wrong. Being *in* BKR is normal. Being in BKR *with a code* is what makes a mortgage adviser wince.

### How long it stays

The standard retention period is five years after the credit has been repaid or the arrears resolved. On 12 February 2026 the Tweede Kamer adopted the Wet stelsel kredietregistratie, which gives BKR a statutory basis, shortens that retention to three years, and obliges providers to inform you within 30 days when an arrears code is registered. It still requires Eerste Kamer approval; until it enters into force, five years remains the rule. If your plans depend on a code disappearing, confirm the current position rather than relying on this article.

### Check your own file, for free

Log in at [mijnkredietregistratie.nl](https://www.mijnkredietregistratie.nl/) using iDIN — the identity service built into your Dutch bank's login. The digital overview of your own data is free. Certified paper statements carry a fee, and you should never pay an intermediary to obtain your file on your behalf. BKR itself warns about phishing messages asking for iDIN details or payments; treat any such email as fraudulent.

Do this once a year, and always before applying for a mortgage. Errors happen: a repaid loan left open, a closed credit card still showing a limit, a handset instalment that was settled years ago.

## What Dutch institutions actually check

### Landlords and letting agents

Private landlords in the Dutch rental market rarely run a credit check. They ask for a copy of your employment contract, recent payslips, a passport or residence document, and sometimes an employer's statement (werkgeversverklaring) or a previous landlord's reference. Many require income of three to four times the monthly rent. Housing corporations for social housing use income ceilings and waiting time instead.

This is why proof of income is the document expats should keep permanently accessible — and why the rental market is where newcomers get exploited. If a "landlord" asks for a credit report, a deposit before viewing, or payment by an unusual method, read our guide to [rental scams in the Netherlands](/guides/housing/rental-scams-netherlands-expats-2026/) before you send anything.

### Mobile providers

A postpaid contract with a subsidised handset is consumer credit. The provider will run a BKR check and, if the financed amount is above €250, register the instalment part. That registration then counts against your borrowing capacity for as long as it runs.

There are two clean ways around it: pay for the handset outright, or take a SIM-only plan and buy the phone separately. ### Banks

Opening a Dutch current account is not a credit decision. The bank runs identity and sanctions checks, needs a BSN for most traditional accounts, and asks about your tax residence. No credit history is involved. If you have not yet opened one, our [step-by-step guide to getting a Dutch bank account](/blog/getting-a-dutch-bank-account-step-by-step-2026/) covers the sequence, and the [comparison of the best accounts for expats](/guides/finance/best-bank-account-expats-netherlands-2026/) covers which one to pick.

Where banks do check BKR is for an overdraft facility, a credit card with a repayment option, or a loan.

### Credit card issuers

Dutch cards split into two groups. Charge cards and cards settled in full each month by direct debit are the norm, and these are treated as a payment product with an implicit short-term limit. Cards with a genuine revolving repayment facility are consumer credit and are registered.

For expats, the practical value of a Dutch card is not history-building: it is deposits at car rental desks, hotel guarantees, and travel purchase protection. ### Mortgage lenders

This is the moment your file actually matters, and the weighting is worth stating plainly. Lenders look at gross annual income against national affordability standards, employment type and duration, the length of your residence permit relative to the loan, your own funds for the buying costs that the mortgage will not finance, and your BKR file. A clean file is a pass mark, not a bonus. An A-code will usually mean a refusal from mainstream lenders while it stands.

Two expat-specific points recur. A temporary contract or freelance income does not disqualify you, but it changes the documentation: lenders typically want a longer track record and an income calculation from a recognised body. And student debt at DUO reduces capacity even though it is not a BKR entry, so declare it — lenders ask, and the amount is verifiable.

## A realistic build plan for your first three years

Since there is no score, the goal is a *documented, boring, verifiable* financial life. That is what a Dutch underwriter rewards.

**Month one.** Register with your gemeente and get your BSN, because almost everything downstream depends on it. Open a Dutch current account in your own name and have your salary paid into it. Take out the mandatory basisverzekering if you are insured under the Dutch system. Keep digital copies of your contract and first payslips.

**Months two to six.** Move recurring commitments — rent, health insurance, utilities, phone — onto direct debit from the Dutch account, and keep a buffer so nothing ever bounces. A reversed direct debit is not a BKR event, but repeated reversals do generate administrative fees, chasing letters and eventually collection costs, which is the actual route to trouble in the Dutch system.

**Months six to twelve.** Check your BKR file once via iDIN so you know what is there. Close any credit facility you are not using, including unused overdraft limits and dormant store credit, because open limits reduce mortgage capacity. Start a separate savings buffer: for a house purchase you will need cash for transfer tax where it applies, notary and valuation costs, and adviser fees.

**Year two.** If a mortgage is the goal, focus on contract stability rather than on credit products. If you are self-employed, keep clean annual accounts from the first year — freelancers are assessed on filed figures, so our [ZZP tax guide](/guides/finance/tax-freelancers-zzp-netherlands-2026/) is more relevant to your future mortgage than any card. If your residence permit is short-dated, note that lenders look at its duration and at your prospects of renewal.

**Year three.** Pull your BKR overview again before you speak to a mortgage adviser, and correct anything stale. Bring: passport and residence document, employment contract, three recent payslips, an employer's statement, an annual income statement (jaaropgave), DUO statement if applicable, and proof of savings.

## Five myths worth dropping

**"I need a credit card to build credit."** You do not. A card with a revolving facility creates a registration that reduces borrowing capacity. Get a card for travel and deposits if you need one, not as a credit-building exercise.

**"My US or UK score will transfer."** It will not. There is no data-sharing route, and Dutch lenders have no framework for interpreting a foreign score. Occasionally a foreign report helps in a manual conversation with a specialist adviser; treat it as colour, not evidence.

**"Paying rent on time builds my file."** Rent is not registered in the Netherlands. Paying on time protects you from collections, which is worth doing for its own sake, but it produces no positive record.

**"A BKR registration ruins my mortgage chances."** Only a code does. Many homeowners have had ordinary registrations. Get your own overview and see what you are actually dealing with before assuming the worst.

**"A company can remove my BKR registration for a fee."** Be very careful here. A registration can only be corrected if it is factually wrong or was recorded in breach of the rules, and you can dispute it yourself with the provider and then with BKR at no cost. Paid "removal" services cannot delete an accurate entry.

## The honest summary

Building credit in the Netherlands is mostly a matter of not needing to. The system asks for a stable income, a Dutch account, commitments that are paid on schedule, and a credit register that is either empty or clean. There is no score to game, no card to open for the sake of it, and no ladder to climb — which is genuinely good news for newcomers, because it means the biggest lever is the one you already control: your employment documentation and your buffer.

Check your file for free once a year, close credit lines you are not using, and put your energy into contract stability and savings. That is the whole strategy.
